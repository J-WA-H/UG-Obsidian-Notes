Potential:
$$V(r)=-\frac{4\alpha_{s}}{3r}+\beta r$$
Includes coulomb potential term that dominates at short distances and shows the gluon exchange and linear confining potential that dominates at large distances. $\beta$ is known as the string tension and is $\approx0.18\text{ GeV}^2$. We use natural units where $\hbar=c=1$.

The linear terms comes from the interaction between the gluons themselves. Unlike photons, gluons can interact on there own so as we measure interactions further away, we are enclosing a larger number of gluons so the interaction between them gets stronger. This interaction is why its hard to separate quarks and is responsible for confinement. 
#### Colour
Colour describes a quantum state that quarks have to allow them to exist within the same particle without breaking the Pauli exclusion principle. Overall, baryons and mesons are colourless, and quarks have colours red, green and blue, and anti-quarks have anti-red, anti-green and anti-blue colours. These colours add in the same way as RGB values, with white being colourless.

The force between quarks is known as the colour force, and can be seen as the source of the strong force between baryons. The colour force does not drop off with distance, as the above potential shows and is responsible for the confinement of quarks. The colour force involves the exchange of gluons.

Quark confinement is a consequence of the colour force - which means that we cannot observe an isolated quark. To separate a quark the energy required would produce a quark-antiquark pair before the the quarks are separate enough to be observed.
