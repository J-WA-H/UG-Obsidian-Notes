A two particle wavefunction is represented by $\Psi(\underline{r},\sigma;\underline{r}',\sigma)=\Psi(1,2)$ with Hamiltonian $\hat{H}(\underline{r},\sigma;\underline{r}',\sigma')=\hat{H}(1,2)$.
Now define the interchange operator $\hat{P}_{12}$ that swaps the labels 1,2.
$$\hat{P}_{12}\Psi(1,2)=\Psi(2,1)$$
Also $\hat{P}_{12}^2=\mathbb{I}\implies \hat{P}_{12}=\hat{P}_{12}^{-1}$.
Acting $\hat{P}_{12}$ on wavefunctions yield
$$\hat{P}_{12}\Psi(\underline{r}_{1},\sigma_{1})=\Psi(\underline{r}_{2},\sigma_{2})$$
$$\hat{P}_{12}\alpha(\sigma_{1})=\alpha(\sigma_{2}).$$
Acting $\hat{P}_{12}$ on the Hamiltonian operator for two identical particles
$$\hat{P}_{12}\hat{H}(1,2)\hat{P}_{!2}^{-1}=\hat{H}(2,1)$$
$$\hat{P}_{12}\hat{H}(1,2)\hat{P}_{12}^{-1}\hat{P}_{12}=\hat{H}(2,1)\hat{P}_{12}$$
$$\hat{P}_{12}\hat{H}(1,2)=\hat{H}(2,1)\hat{P}_{12}.$$As the two particles are identical, $\hat{H}(1,2)=\hat{H}(2,1)$
$$\hat{P}_{12}\hat{H}(1,2)=\hat{H}(1,2)\hat{P}_{12}$$
$$\implies[\hat{P}_{12},\hat{H}(1,2)]=0.$$
As these operators commute, they share a common set of eigenfunctions.
$$\hat{P}_{12}\Psi(1,2)=\lambda\Psi(1,2)$$
$$\hat{P}_{12}\hat{P}_{12}\Psi(1,2)=\lambda \hat{P}_{12}\Psi(1,2)$$
$$\Psi(1,2)=\lambda^2\Psi(1,2)$$
$$\implies \lambda=\pm 1.$$
Identical particles fall into 2 categories:
$$\lambda=+1\to\text{ Bosons}$$$$\lambda=-1\to\text{ Fermions}.$$
