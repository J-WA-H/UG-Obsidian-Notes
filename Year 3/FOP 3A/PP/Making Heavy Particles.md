One way to produce heavy particles is via an $e^{+}e^{-}$ collision at very high energies,
![[Pasted image 20250313111318.png]]
Aligning the momentum along the $z$ axis,
$$p_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}
E_{1} \\
\underline{p}_{1}
\end{bmatrix};\;p_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}
E_{2} \\
\underline{p}_{2}
\end{bmatrix}.$$
Evaluate in the centre of mass frame,
$$E=E_{1}-E_{2},\;p_{x}=p_{y}=0,\;p_{z}=p_{z_{1}}=-p_{z_{2}}.$$
Define
$$S=(p_{1}+p_{2})^2=(E+E,\underline{0})=4E^2\implies \sqrt{ S }=2E.$$
As long as the mass of the right hand side is $<E$, then this interaction can happen.

CERN used to do these collisions at around $2E\sim 300-350\text{ GeV}$. The mass of the top quark is $\sim 175\text{ GeV}$ so it can just about be produced.

This can produce either 2 or 3 mesons.

Baryon production:
![[Pasted image 20250313112427.png]]
This produces a proton and an anti-proton when there is enough energy to bind the quarks together rather than produce $q\bar{q}$ pairs.

To produce heavier particles than $e^{+}e^{ - }$ collisions can make, heavier particles are used in collisions instead.
e.g. proton-antiproton collisions
![[Pasted image 20250313112726.png]]
The collisions of, say an up quark and anti-down, produces a W boson so this is a $W^{\pm}$ factory.

However, it is difficult to get a high enough abundance of antiprotons to observe enough $W^{\pm}$ bosons so proton-proton collisions are used instead. These are matter-matter collisions that can accelerate to $\sqrt{ S }\sim 14\text{ TeV}$.

As matter-matter collisions don't produce anything, the gluons are what interacts, producing $q\bar{q}$ pairs,
![[Pasted image 20250313113316.png]]
At high enough energies, can produce a top anti-top pair.
![[Pasted image 20250313113423.png]]
As Higgs couples to mass, and top quarks are the heaviest particles, $t\bar{t}$ decays into a Higgs boson.

Proton-proton collisions gives a Higgs factory.
