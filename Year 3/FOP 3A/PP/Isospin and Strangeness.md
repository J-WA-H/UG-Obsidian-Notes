We have seen that [[Mesons]] have [parity](Conserved%20Quantities.md) 
$$P(q\bar{q})=P(q)P(\bar{q})(-1)^{L}=(-1)^{L+1}.$$
It also total [[Angular Momentum]]
$$|L-S|\leq J\leq L+S.$$
These two quantities are used to label a meson:
$$J^{P}.$$
Consider a series of $S$ state mesons,
For a spin 0, even parity meson,
$$0^{+}$$
This is a scalar meson.

For a spin 0, odd parity meson,
$$0^-$$
This is a pseudoscalar meson.

For a spin 1, odd parity meson,
$$1^{-}$$
This is a vector meson.

For a spin 1, even parity meson,
$$1^{+}$$
This is a pseudo/axial vector meson.

These names for the type of mesons are analogous to [[Pseudo-Scalars and Axial Vectors]].

Recall that when [[Measuring 2 spin-half particles]], we get symmetric triplet states and an antisymmetric singlet state
Triplet
$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{ 2 }}(\ket{\uparrow\downarrow} +\ket{\downarrow\uparrow} ),\;\ket{\uparrow\uparrow} ,\;\ket{\downarrow\downarrow} .$$
Singlet
$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{ 2 }}(\ket{\uparrow\downarrow}-\ket{\downarrow\uparrow}  ).$$

This analogy can be applied for up and down quarks. The physics is slightly different but the results are the same.
Triplet,
$$-\ket{u\bar{d}} ,\; \frac{1}{\sqrt{ 2 }}(\ket{u\bar{u}} -\ket{d\bar{d}} ),\;\ket{d\bar{u}} $$
and the singlet,
$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{ 2 }}(\ket{u\bar{u}} +\ket{d\bar{d}} ).$$
Like how the spin singlet/triplets have $S$ and $M$ values - the mesons also have a quantity analogous to this - charge.
For the triplet, $\ket{u\bar{d}}$ has charge 1, $\frac{1}{\sqrt{ 2 }}(\ket{u\bar{u}}-\ket{d\bar{d}})$ has charge 0, and $\ket{d\bar{u}}$ has charge -1.

Heisenberg introduced the concept of isospin.

Isospin is a vector. In quantum mechanics, we usually choose an axis to align the vector along the $z$ axis.
The third component of isospin is $I_{z}=I_{3}$,
$$I_{3}=\begin{cases}
+\frac{1}{2} & \text{up} \\
-\frac{1}{2} & \text{down} \\
0 & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}.$$
The total isospin is given by half the difference of the number of up quarks and the number of down quarks.
Antiquarks swap the sign of isospin - antiup has isospin $-\frac{1}{2}$ and antidown has isospin $+\frac{1}{2}$.

Example: Delta baryons,

|         | $\Delta^{++}(uuu)$ | $\Delta^+(uud)$ | $\Delta^{0}(udd)$ | $\Delta^{-}(ddd)$ |
| ------- | ------------------ | --------------- | ----------------- | ----------------- |
| $I_{3}$ | $\frac{3}{2}$      | $\frac{1}{2}$   | $-\frac{1}{2}$    | $-\frac{3}{2}$    |
These differ by 1 - consistent with any vector in quantum mechanics aligned along the $z$ axis.
This group has total isospin $I=\frac{3}{2}$ with components $-I\leq I_{3}\leq I$.
