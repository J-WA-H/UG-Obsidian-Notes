The strength of a particle interaction is given by the product of the vertices of the [[Feynman Diagrams]] and the propagator, defined as
$$\frac{1}{q^2-m^2+i\Gamma}.$$
$\Gamma$ is the lifetime, $q^2$ is the momentum transfer and $m$ is the mass.
The vertices are described by by their strength, $\alpha$ - the fine structure constant - $\alpha_{s}$ - the strong structure constant - and $\alpha_{w}$ - the weak structure constant.

This interaction is mediated by a photon, so each vertex has strength $\alpha$. The total strength of this interaction is hence given by
$$\frac{\alpha^2}{q^2+i\Gamma}.$$
The mass term has been omitted as photons are massless. If this was mediated by the weak force, the $Z^{0},W^{+},W^{-}$ bosons have large mass so this interaction strength of the weak force is weak.

The strong force is the strongest with relative strength $1$, then the EM force with relative strength $10^{-2}$, then the weak force with strength $10^{-6}$.
