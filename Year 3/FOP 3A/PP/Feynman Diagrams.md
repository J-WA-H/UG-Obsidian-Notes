Feynman diagrams formalise a way to draw a particle interaction. 
The bottom axis is a time axis, representing the interaction's evolution in time.

Fermions are drawn with a line and an arrow moving forwards in time $\rightarrow$.

Anti-fermions are drawn with an arrow in the opposite direction moving backwards in time $\leftarrow$.

The exchange interaction shows which particle emits and which absorbs the [virtual particle](Virtual%20Particles.md). The left diagram shows the anti-particle emitting the virtual particle first then the particle absorbs it at a later time - while the right diagram shows the other way round.

These diagrams show that particles are drawn moving forwards in time and anti-particles are represented as moving backwards in time.

The virtual particle is drawn differently depending on the force mediator.

Diagrams are made of vertices - where there is an interception of 3 lines. This gives the strength of the interaction, $\alpha=\frac{1}{137}$ for EM scattering, $\alpha_{s}$ for strong scattering, and $\alpha_{w}$ for weak scattering.
