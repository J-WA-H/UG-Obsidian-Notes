Recall from [[Rutherford experiment]] that the Rutherford cross section is
$$\left( \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} \right)_{Ruth}=\frac{(eZ_{1})^2(eZ_{2})^2}{4E^2\sin^4 \frac{\theta}{2}}.$$
The cross section, $\sigma$, gives the relative size of a target where the incoming particle will collide with it.
![[Pasted image 20250118113414.png]]
In scattering there is an exchange of energy/momentum.

Rutherford's experiment is in the classical regime but in particle physics, particles are quantum and relativistic.

The relativistic regime requires magnetism to describe interactions. 

The [[Mott Cross Section]] includes relativity and magnetism, done by accounting for the spins of the particles.
$$\left( \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} \right)_{Mott}=\left( \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} \right)_{Ruth}\left( 1-\beta^2\sin^2 \frac{\theta}{2} \right)$$
where $\beta=\frac{v}{c}$.
This returns the Rutherford cross section in the small $v$ limit. In the high $v$ limit, $\beta\to 1$ so
$$\left( \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} \right)_{Mott}=\left( \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} \right)_{Ruth}\cos^2 \frac{\theta}{2}.$$
