Heavy quarks (c,b,t) are unstable with respect to the decay of lighter particles and can be in [[Mesons]] $q\bar{q}$ in [states](Notation%20for%20Bound%20States.md) $n^{2S+1}L_{J}$.

The possible decays are electromagnetic, strong, and weak decay. The route of decay is dependent on the state as the different [conservation rules](Conserved%20Quantities.md) have to be followed.

Consider the ground state of the charmonium $c\bar{c}$ $1^{1}S_{0}$ and the first excited state $1^{3}S_{1}$. Consider the electromagnetic decay of these states

![[Pasted image 20250221094434.png]]
For the $1^3S_{1}$, it has total angular momentum 1 so it can decay into a photon with $J=1$ which then can decay into a particle, antiparticle pair - which has to have $J=1$.
![[Pasted image 20250221094322.png]]
For $1^1S_{0}$, the original state has total angular momentum 0 and it decays into a photon which has total angular momentum 1, which breaks conservation of angular momentum so this decay is not possible. 

The $1^{1}S_{0}$ state can decay by other routes that conserve angular momentum. For example, it can decay into 2 photons which, through addition of quantum angular momentum, have $J=0,1,2$ so it can decay into the photons as long as they combine to give $J=0$.
![[Pasted image 20250221094750.png]]
Now consider the strong decay of the $1^{1}S_{0}$ $c\bar{c}$ state,
![[Pasted image 20250221095010.png]]
If the charm quark has a [[Colour Charge]] of red, the anti-charm has a colour charge of anti-red - so following through the flow of colour charge gives the mediator [gluon](Gluons.md) having a charge of $r\bar{r}$ which is white so this quark cannot exist - meaning this decay into one gluon is impossible.

Consider the decay into 3 gluons,
![[Pasted image 20250221095256.png]]
Following though colour charge gives 3 non-white gluons - each producing a particle-antiparticle pair. However, following through colour charge gives the $q\bar{q}$ pairs as being coloured - which is not allowed so the 3 quarks combine to give a white baryon $q_{1}q_{2}q_{3}$ with colour $rgb$,  and the 3 anti-quarks combine to give an anti baryon $\bar{q}_{1}\bar{q}_{2}\bar{q}_{3}$ with colour $\bar{g} \bar{b}\bar{r}$.
This shows that heavy meson decay leaves 3 trails.

Another strong decay for $1^{3}S_{1}$ ($J/\psi$) could be the anticharm quark emitting a gluon, while the charm quark remains unchanged
![[Pasted image 20250221100249.png]]
This gluon produces a particle-antiparticle pair, $d\bar{d}$ or a $u\bar{u}$ which are the only possible pairs due to the energy of the $1^{3}S_{1}$ state.
This $q\bar{q}$ pair is coloured so instead, to produce white particles, the quark combines with the anti-charm and the antiquark combines with the charm quark. The $d\bar{c}$ pair is the $D^{-}$ particle, the $c\bar{d}$ pair is the $D^{+}$ particle, the $u\bar{c}$ pair is the $D^{0}$ particle and $c\bar{u}$ is the $\bar{D}^{0}$ particle. This shows meson decay is not unique so the resulting particle are a quantum mechanical combination of the two possible decays.
$$J/\psi\to \frac{1}{\sqrt{ 2 }}[(\ket{D^{+}} +\ket{D^{-}} )+(\ket{D^{0}} +\ket{\bar{D}^{0}} )].$$
These are the only possible results as $M_{c\bar{c}}>M_{D^{0}}+M_{\bar{D}^{0}}$ and $M_{c\bar{c}}>M_{D^{-}}+M_{D^{+}}$, but there is not enough energy for the gluon to produce a $s\bar{s}$ pair, so this and above are not possible.

Consider the weak decay of charmonium. 
![[Pasted image 20250221134108.png]]
Consider the case where the anticharm quark remains unchanged while the charm quark emits a $W^{+}$ boson. As the $W^{+}$ has a charge of +1, so the charm quark decays into another quark with charge $-\frac{1}{3}$. Due to conservation of energy the charm quark must decrease a generation so it decays to the down quark.
From conservation of charge, the $W^{+}$ must produce a pair that has a charge of +1, for example a $e^{+}$ and $\nu_e$. There is an electron neutrino to conserve lepton number within a generation.

It must be noted that photons, gluons and $Z^{0}$ do not change the flavour of the quark while $W^{+}$ and $W^{-}$ do.
