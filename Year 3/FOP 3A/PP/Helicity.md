Helicity is given by
$$h=\frac{{\underline{s}\cdot \underline{p}}}{|\underline{s}||\underline{p}|}.$$
This relates spin and 3-momentum.

Helicity must be conserved in a particle interaction. 

Consider a scattering of an electron to a proton, with spin aligned parallel to the motion of the electron.
![[Pasted image 20250118131007.png]]
As spin and momentum are parallel, $\underline{s}\cdot \underline{p}=|\underline{s}||\underline{p}|$, so the helicity of this interaction is $h=1$. Consider the back scattering scenario:
![[Pasted image 20250118131248.png]]
Here since the spin is unaltered and the momentum flips, $\underline{s}\cdot \underline{p}=-|\underline{s}||\underline{p}|$, so $h=-1$, which is not allowed as helicity isn't conserved.
![[Pasted image 20250118131427.png]]
For helicity to be conserved, the spin of the electron must flip, where the flip is induced by the magnetic field of the interaction. As this interaction has $h=1$, it is allowed as it conserves helicity.

Helicity may not be conserved in different reference frames.

This gives the [scattering formula](Cross-Sections.md) for a charge and magnetic distribution - the Rosenbluth formula,
$$\left( \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} \right)_{Ros}=\left( \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} \right)_{Mott}\left[ \frac{G_{E}^2(Q^2)+\tau G_{M}^2(Q^2)}{1+\tau} +2\tau G_{M}^2(Q^2)\tan^2 \frac{\theta}{2}\right]$$
where $G_{E}(Q^2)$ is the Fourier transform of the electric field and $Q_{M}(Q^2)$ is the Fourier transform of the magnetic field.
