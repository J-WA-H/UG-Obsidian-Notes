Consider the [[Feynman Diagrams]] for $u\bar{u}$ scattering,
![[Pasted image 20250207100638.png]]
These diagrams can be labelled with the quarks [[Colour Charge]], arbitrarily assigning the $u$ quark a red charge and the $\bar{u}$ anti-quark an anti-green charge.
![[Pasted image 20250207102155.png]]
An anti-particle is like a particle moving backwards in time. Looking at the flow of red charge in the first diagram, at some point it absorbs a gluon, and for the flow of red the gluon moves backwards in time - giving it an anti red charge.
Similarly, looking at the flow of green charge, the gluon appears to move forward in time so it also carries a green charge.

This gives colour flow, and as a result a gluon carries two colour charges, one colour and one anti-colour.
If the gluon moves forward in time, it carries a colour, and if it moves backwards in time, it carries an anti-colour.

Normally we don't know the time order of the interaction so this gluon has two possible colour charges, $\bar{r}g$ or $r\bar{g}$.
This gives 9 possible combinations of gluons: $r\bar{r},r\bar{g},r\bar{b},g\bar{r},g\bar{g},g\bar{b},b\bar{r},b\bar{g},b\bar{b}$.

Experimentally we observe no white gluons - white particles are free and coloured particles are bound inside another particle - there are no free particles that are not white.

The strong force is very short ranged, so the individual colours can only exist within these bound particles. If a non-white particle existed, this would imply colour and the strong force has to be long ranged.

As strong scattering is much stronger than electromagnetic scattering, a white gluon could move long distances and overwhelm the electromagnetic force from photons - meaning that white gluons do not exist.
Looking at the 9 gluons, it appears that $r\bar{r},g\bar{g},b\bar{b}$ are white so do not exist - which would imply there are 6 gluons, but in reality there are 8. This is where the colour analogy breaks down.

The 8 gluons are a quantum mechanical combination of the 9 possible gluons.

In quantum mechanics, 9 particles can be expressed as a 9x9 matrix - the eigenvectors are the gluons, one is white and the rest are not which gives 8 possible states.
By analogy with spin, we have the Pauli matrices, giving the possible combinations of $\uparrow\downarrow$ spin. Colour works the same way - the gluon states are described by 8 3x3 Gell-Mann matrices.

Considering the propagator in the scattering event, mathematically it is given by
$$\frac{1}{q^2-M+i\Gamma}.$$
This can be used to define a matrix element to describe the scattering event,
$$\frac{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}{q^2-M+i\Gamma}$$
where $\alpha_{i}$ is the interaction strength at the $i^{th}$ vertex.

Consider photon and gluon scattering,
![[Pasted image 20250207141642.png]]
Photons couple to charge, and as photons don't carry charge, they don't interact so the value of the vertex is zero.
Gluons carry colour charge, so they do interact with each other and there is a flow of colour, so the value of the vertex is non-zero.

Because of this interaction, the majority of the [proton's](Proton%20Structure.md) mass comes from the binding energy between the constituent quarks, and not the quarks themselves.
