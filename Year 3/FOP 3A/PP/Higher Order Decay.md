The order of a [diagram](Feynman%20Diagrams.md) corresponds to how many force mediators are involved in an interaction.
![[Pasted image 20250306154406.png]]
1st order diagrams involve one force mediator.
![[Pasted image 20250306154532.png]]
2 force mediators are second order diagrams, like this electron scattering interaction.

As you add more vertices, the fine structure constant brings the probability down as each vertex divides by $\approx 100$. The higher the order of the interaction, the less likely it is.

Examples:
Decay of $Z^{0}$,
![[Pasted image 20250306154818.png]]
or an electron passing through time
![[Pasted image 20250306154850.png]]
This emits and reabsorbs photons as it moves, with each photon having the possibility to produce an particle - antiparticle pair which then annihilates which produces another photon which is reabsorbed by the electron.

Consider the [decay](Light%20Meson%20Decay.md) of kaons.
$$K^{0}(d\bar{s})\to \pi^{-}+\pi^{+}$$
![[Pasted image 20250306155235.png]]
$$K^{0}\to \pi^{-}+e^{+}+\nu_{e}$$
![[Pasted image 20250306155310.png]]
The mass of $K^{0}$ is greater than the mass of 3 pions so there is a possible decay route from this.
$$K^{0}\to \pi^{+}+\pi^{-}+\pi^{0}$$
![[Pasted image 20250306155434.png]]
For the decay of $K^{-}(s\bar{u})$, it has many decay routes, for example,
$$K^{-}\to \mu^{-}+\bar{\nu}_{\mu}$$
$$K^{-}\to \pi^{+}$$
![[Pasted image 20250306155752.png]]
$$K^{-}\to \pi^{-}+\pi^{0}$$
![[Pasted image 20250306155806.png]]
As kaons are fairly heavy with respect to pions, it is fairly common for it to decay so the second order decay is most likely.
