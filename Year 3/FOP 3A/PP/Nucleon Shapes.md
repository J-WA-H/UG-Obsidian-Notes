Consider some charge density distribution $\rho(r)$ and a Fourier transform $\mathcal{F}(q)$ where $q$ is the transfer momentum. We get the modification of the [Mott cross-section](Cross-Sections.md) cross-section
$$\left( \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} \right)_{\rho}=\left( \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} \right)_{Mott}|\mathcal{F}(q)|^2.$$
This introduces a $q$ dependence on the cross-section.
For a point charge, the charge density is a delta function, so the Fourier transform is a constant.

Now smear out the charge distribution and take its Fourier transform:

As small energy/$q$ values, the Fourier transform looks constant so it is impossible to distinguish between a point charge and a charge distribution. 

Consider a solid sphere with uniform charge density.
![[Pasted image 20250118123647.png]]
The Fourier transform at small $q$ values is also roughly constant.

This means when a charge is observed at small energies, it is observed to be a point charge.
