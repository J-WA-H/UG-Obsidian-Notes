Consider the [rotation](Rotations.md) 
$$\begin{bmatrix}
x \\
y
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}
\cos \theta & \sin \theta \\
-\sin \theta & \cos \theta
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}
x' \\
y'
\end{bmatrix}.$$
$$\underline{x}^T\underline{x}=\underline{x}'^T\underline{\underline{R}}^T\underline{\underline{R}}\underline{x}'=\underline{x}'^T\underline{x}'.$$
Energy and momentum are variants - between frames they are linked by the [Lorentz transformation](Relativistic%20Kinematics.md). The total energy and momentum are conserved if if different observers measure them to be different. 

The Minkowski scalar product is invariant under a Lorentz transformation and is defined by
$$v^2=(v_{9})^2-\lvert \underline{v} \rvert^2=v^{\mu}\eta_{\mu \nu}v^{\nu}=v^Tg_{\mu \nu}v. $$
$E^2$ and $p^2$ are invariants. Using [[Natural Units]]:
$$E^2=m^2+p^2$$
$$p^2=p_{\mu}p^{\mu}=E^2-\lvert \underline{p} \rvert^2=m^2+p^2-p^2=m^2. $$
All observers agree on the rest mass so $p^2$ is invariant in all frames, hence so is $E^2$.
