The force that keeps protons bound in a nucleus is the strong force. It acts over a short range of about $1\text{ fm}$. We can measure it by [[Scattering]] a proton at a neutron, this way we can observe the proton but there is no electromagnetic interaction at large distances dwarfing the strong force.

We denote an atom as$${}^{A}_{Z}\mathbf{X}$$where $A$ is the atomic number - the total number of nucleons in the atom, and $Z$ is the number of protons.
 
The mass of a proton is $m_{p}=938.27\text{ MeV}=1.0073\text{ u}$ and the mass of a neutron is $m_{n}=939.57\text{ MeV}=1.0087\text{ u}$.
$u$ is the atomic unit and is defined as 1/12 of the mass of a carbon-12 nucleus.

For ${}^4_2\mathbf{He}$, we would think that the mass of the nucleus would be the sum of the masses of 2 protons and two neutrons, $4.032\text{ u}$, however the observed mass is lower, at $4.015\text{ u}$. This shows that the process of binding particles together to form a nucleus releases some energy, a photon of energy $\approx 0.017\text{ u}$.

The total mass of a nucleus is given by
$$M(A,Z)=Z(m_{p}+m_{e})+(A-Z)m_{n}-B(A,Z)$$
where $B(A,Z)$ is the binding energy.
The binding energy is given by the drop-model formula
$$B(A,Z)=a_{V}A-a_{S}A^{2/3}-a_{c} \frac{Z^2}{A^{1/3}}-a_{a} \frac{(N-Z)^2}{4A}+\frac{\delta}{A^{1/2}}.$$

The volume term, $a_{V}A$, accounts for the short range of the strong interaction, nucleons like to surround themselves.
The surface term, $-a_{s}A^{2/3}$, is to correct for the nucleons on the surface of the nucleus.
![[Pasted image 20241128184528.png]]
The Coulomb term, $-aZ^2/A^{1/3}$, is to correct for the electromagnetic repulsion between protons.
The asymmetry term, $-a_a(N-Z)^2/4A$ corrects for any imbalance in the number of protons and neutrons, which impacts the nuclear volume due as nucleons must follow Pauli exclusion.
The pairing term, $\delta/A^{1/2}$, accounts for the pairing of two nucleons with different spins. The value of $\delta$ is defined depending on if the number of protons and the number of neutrons is odd or even.
$$\delta=\begin{cases}
\lvert \delta \rvert  & \text{even-even} \\
0 & \text{even-odd} \\
-\lvert \delta \rvert  & \text{odd-odd}
\end{cases}.$$
