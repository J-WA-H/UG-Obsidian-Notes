For a nucleus to decay, it must be energetically viable, so the total energy released, $Q$,  must be follow
$$Q=M_{parent}-\sum_{daughter}m_{i}>0.$$
It must also respect all known conservation laws (charge, lepton number, baryon number).

The number of unstable particles $N(t)$ decreases in time - described by the differential equation
$$\frac{dN}{dt}=-\lambda N\implies N(t)=N(0)e^{-\lambda t}.$$
The mean lifetime is defined as the time taken for the number of particles to decrease by $\frac{1}{e}$.
$$\tau=\frac{1}{\lambda}.$$
The activity is defined as the number of decays per unit time
$$A=-\frac{dN}{dt}.$$
The radiation dose an observer experiences is the energy received by the observer per unit mass, and is measured in Greys.
$$\text{dose}=\frac{E}{m}\text{ Gy}.$$
$1\text{ Gy}=1\text{ J kg}^{-1}$.

The equivalent energy does in Sieverts is
$$H=\bar{\gamma} \frac{E}{m}$$
where
$$\bar{\gamma}=\begin{cases}
1 & \text{photons} \\
1 & \text{electrons} \\
20 & \alpha \text{ particles} \\
3 & \text{neutrons}
\end{cases}.$$
