The cross section for the [[Rutherford experiment]] works for non-relativistic particles, but not in the relativistic regime. To examine subatomic distances, $\lambda \approx 0.1\text{ pm}$, we need a momentum of
$$p=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\approx 13\text{ Mev}$$
using [[Natural Units]].
For an alpha particle with mass $3.7\text{ GeV}$, it is easy to achieve this momentum in the non relativistic regime, but for an electron of mass $0.511\text{ MeV}$, so to relativistic speeds are needed to reach this momentum.

This means for scattering with electrons, the Rutherford cross-section needs to be adjusted to Mott's cross-section, given by
$$\left( \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} \right)_{Mott}=\left( \frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} \right)_{Ruth}\left( 1-v^2\sin^2\left( \frac{\theta}{2} \right) \right).$$
