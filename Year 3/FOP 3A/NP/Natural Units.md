The constants $\hbar=6.587\times 10^{-16}\text{ eVs}$ and $c=3\times 10^{8}\text{ ms}^{-1}$ appear a frequently in nuclear and particle physics. Natural units sets these constants to unity.
$$\hbar=c=1.$$
For example, the Schrodinger equation in these units is
$$i \frac{\partial \ket{\Psi} }{\partial t}=\frac{1}{2m}\nabla^2\ket{\Psi}+V(\underline{r})\ket{\Psi}.$$
We will measure energy, mass and momentum in units of $\text{eV}$ and length in $\text{eV}^{-1}$.
Example:
In natural units the length of some object is $L_{nu}=2\times 10^{-6}\text{ eV}^{-1}$. To convert this into SI units, we need to multiply by powers of $\hbar$ and $c$ - the powers determined by dimensional analysis.
$$[L_{SI}]=[L_{nu}][\hbar]^{\alpha}[c]^{\beta}$$
$$[\text{m}]=[\text{eV}^{-1}][\text{eV s}]^{\alpha}[\text{ms}^{-1}]^{\beta}$$
$$\implies \alpha=1;\beta=1$$
so
$$L_{SI}=L_{nu}\hbar c=100\text{ fm}.$$
