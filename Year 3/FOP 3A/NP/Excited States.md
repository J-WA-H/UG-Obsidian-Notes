For protons inside a nucleus, they fill up from the lowest energy state upwards. For example, for ${}^{15}_{7}N$, the protons fill the states as
![[Pasted image 20241206145437.png]]
The first excited state is when one proton is excited from the $1P_{3/2}$ to the $1P_{1/2}$ states.
However, consider ${}^{16}_{8}O$, where all the states shown above are filled. In this case the first excited state is to the $1d_{5/2}$.

Magic numbers give closed shells - very stable with high binding energy and hard to excite.

In the transition between states, which includes the emission of a photon, must conserve [[Angular Momentum]] and [parity](Symmetries%20and%20Parity.md).
$$\underline{J}_{i}=\underline{J}_{f}+\underline{l}_{\gamma}$$
$$P_{i}=P_{f}P_{\gamma}.$$
The parity of the photon is given by
$$P_{\gamma}=\begin{cases}
\text{electric} & (-1)^{l} \\
\text{magnetic} & (-1)^{l+1}
\end{cases}.$$
The lowest $l$ value is more likely - and the state is more likely to be in the electric field rather than magnetic.
