Consider an isolated neutron, beta decay has it decay into a proton, electron and neutrino, with a [mean lifetime](Radioactivity.md) of $\tau=14\text{ mins }40\text{ s}$
![[Pasted image 20241129152234.png]]
We can see that this is energetically viable.
$$Q=m_{n}-m_{p}-m_{e}=1.3-0.5=0.8\text{ MeV}.$$
This energy radiated cannot be from the strong or electromagnetic force. This new force is the weak force.

$\beta^{-}$ decay is a neutron in a nucleus turning into a proton, electron and an anti-neutrino.
![[Pasted image 20241129152952.png]]
The atomic number remains the same but the proton number increases by one.
$${}_{Z}^{A}X\to{}_{Z+1}^{A}Y+e^{-}+\bar{\nu}.$$
The energy released can be found using the condition that the parent mass must be greater than the sum of the daughter masses, by considering the masses of the protons, neutrons, electrons, and [binding energy](The%20Strong%20Force%20and%20Binding%20Energy.md).
$$Z(m_{p}+m_{e})+Nm_{n}-B(A,Z)>(Z+1)(m_{p}+m_{e})+(N-1)m_{n}-B(A,Z+1).$$
This gives that the change in binding energy is bound by $\Delta B>-0.8\text{ MeV}$.

$\beta^+$ decay is the process where a proton turns into a neutron, positron, and a neutrino.
![[Pasted image 20241129154343.png]]
$${}_{Z}^{A}X\to {}_{Z-1}^{A}Y+e^{+}+\nu.$$
For this decay to be viable,
$$M_{ncl}(A,Z)>M_{ncl}(A,Z-1)+m_{e}.$$
Electron capture is the process where an electron is captured by a nucleus which turns a proton into a neutron.
![[Pasted image 20241129155501.png]]
$$e^{-}+{}_{Z}^{A}X\to {}_{Z-1}^{A}Y+\nu.$$
$$m_{e}-\varepsilon+M_{ncl}(A,Z)>M_{ncl}(A,Z-1)$$
where $\varepsilon$ is the binding energy of the electron.
