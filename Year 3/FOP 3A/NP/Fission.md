Fission is the splitting a nucleus into roughly equal parts. Spontaneous fission is a rare process so most of the time we have to induce it, by providing enough energy to overcome the potential barrier.

The left side of the graph acts as a well due to the [strong force](The%20Strong%20Force%20and%20Binding%20Energy.md) so, due to quantum tunnelling, spontaneous emission can occur.
![[Pasted image 20241206100237.png]]
The exponential decay of the amplitude of the wavefunction during tunnelling is given by the Gamow factor:
$$\Psi(x)\propto e^{-G}$$
$$G=\int dx\sqrt{ 2m(V(x)-E) }.$$
In nuclear power, the fission reaction is the decay of ${}^{235}_{92}U$ triggered by a neutron:
$$n+{}^{235}_{92}U\to {}^{141}_{56}Ba+{}^{92}_{26}Kr+3n.$$
As this process releases three neutrons, it is a self-sufficient process as it can trigger 3 more reactions, causing a chain-reaction.
