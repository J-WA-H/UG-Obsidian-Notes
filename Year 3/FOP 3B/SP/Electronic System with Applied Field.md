We have looked at the partition function for a [2-state system](Partition%20Function%20for%20Distinguishable%20Particles.md) with an applied $B$ field with energies $\pm \frac{1}{2}\mu B$.  We shall now scale this up to the bulk system. 
![[Pasted image 20241127142105.png]]![[Pasted image 20241127142126.png]]
We can see that the applied field shifts the Fermi-energy, and we can see that one spin state has elections pushed up above the original Fermi energy and the other spin state has an unoccupied region. Removing the $B$ field allows electrons to move from the higher energy to the lower one by flipping spins, causing an imbalance in the spin, which creates a magnet.

The energy of this system is 
$$U=\int_{0}^{\infty} \varepsilon g(\varepsilon)f_{FD}(\varepsilon)d\varepsilon.$$
Where the [[Fermi-Dirac Distribution]] has its fermi energy shifted by $\pm \frac{1}{2}\mu B$.

This gives the energy supplied by the field to create a paramagnet.
