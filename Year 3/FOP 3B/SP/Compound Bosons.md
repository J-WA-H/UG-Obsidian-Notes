[[Fermions]] are bound by the Pauli exclusion principle so cannot occupy the same state while [[Bosons]] are not bound by this so they all collapse into the ground state.

In a material, we can partition the system into the nucleus + core electrons and valance electrons.

Let's imagine that we smear out a positive nucleus + core and have a homogeneous background charge-homogeneous gas.
![[Pasted image 20241128114334.png]]
![[Pasted image 20241128114353.png]]
The probability of finding an electron giving at the same position as the reference electron is non-zero in the homogeneous case, implying we can bring an electron as close as we like - which violates Pauli exclusion.
![[Pasted image 20241128114635.png]]
An electron in a material is actually an electron plus its exchange hole which is positive - this "quasi-particles" are effectively neutral, this is why the free electron model works.

Consider the whole atom (nucleus + core + valence):
![[Pasted image 20241128115234.png]]
As an electron passes through the system, the nucleus + core is attracted to it - creating a phonon. The fast moving electron leaves a track of positive nuclei attracted to where it was, leaving a track of positive charge where the electron once was.

We have just described the electron-phonon coupling. We gave pairs of electrons attracted to each other. This is called a Cooper pair. 

Higher temperatures induces more phonons which breaks the attractive mechanism. Paired electrons couple to form a boson, and fall into the single lowest energy state of the system. This is BCS superconductivity.
