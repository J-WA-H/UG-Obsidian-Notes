At some to measure particles in a system we go from classical to quantum. First we will look at the extremes in the quantum and classical regimes.
Fermions:
For fermions the number of [microstates](Fermi-Dirac%20Distribution.md) is $$\Omega_{FG}=\frac{g_{j}!}{n_{j}!(g_{j}-n_{j})!}$$
Let's imagine there are many more states than particles:
$$\Omega=\frac{\cancel{1\cdot{2}\cdot 3\cdot \dots \cdot (n_{j}-g_{j})}(n_{j}-g_{j}+1)\dots g_{j}}{n_{j}!\cancel{(g_{j}-n_{j})!}}=\frac{(n_{j}-g_{j}+1)\dots g_{j}}{n_{j}!}$$
as there are many more states than particles, $n_j-g_j+1\approx g_j$ which gives
$$\Omega=\frac{g_{j}^{n_{j}}}{n_{j}!}$$
so for many states
$$\Omega_{FD}(\epsilon)=\prod_{j} \frac{g_{j}^{n_{j}}}{n_{j}!}.$$
This result also holds for bosons and classical particles, which can be shown using the same method.
