From [[Bloch's Theorem]], $\psi(\underline{r})=U(\underline{r})e^{i\underline{k}\cdot \underline{r}}$. From this it can be shown that 
$$\underline{F}=\hbar \frac{dk}{dt}.$$
Using Newton's second law, we have
$$\underline{F}=m^{*}\underline{a}$$
where $m^{*}$ is the effective mass, it takes into account electrons moving through the solid with a periodic potential.

For an isotropic solid,
$$m^{*}=\hbar^2\left( \frac{d^2E}{dk^2} \right)^{-1}.$$
For an anisotropic solid, effective mass becomes a tensor
$$m_{ij}^{*}=\hbar^2\left( \frac{\partial^2E}{\partial k_{i}\partial k_{j}} \right)^{-1}.$$
Applying this to [[Nearly-Free Electron Theory]]:
![[Pasted image 20250122160149.png]]
Towards, the centre of the Brillouin zone, there is a minimum so
$$\frac{d^2E}{dk^2}>0\implies m^{*}>0.$$
Towards the Brillouin zone boundary, there is a maximum so
$$\frac{d^2E}{dk^2}<0\implies m^{*}<0.$$
The negative effective mass implies a deceleration. As you approach the Brillouin zone boundary, the group velocity approaches zero due to the potential of the atom.
