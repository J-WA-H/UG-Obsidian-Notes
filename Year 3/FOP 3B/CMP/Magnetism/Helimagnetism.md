In helimagnets the spin rotates by an angle $\theta$ between successive basal planes. Here the [exchange constant](Exchange%20Interaction.md) between spins in neighbouring planes are important.
![[Pasted image 20250130155243.png]]
Assuming equal spins, the nearest neighbour gives energy
$$E_{1}=-J_{1}\underline{s}_{1}\cdot \underline{s}_{2}=-2NJ_{1}s^2\cos\theta$$
where $N$ is the number of atoms in the basal plane.
The second nearest neighbour contributes an energy of
$$E_{2}=-2NJ_{2}s^2\cos{2}\theta.$$
This gives a total energy of
$$E=-2Ns^2(J_{1}\cos \theta+J_{2}\cos 2\theta).$$
For this to be minimised,
$$\frac{dE}{d\theta}=0=2Ns^2\sin\theta(J_{1}+4J_{2}\cos \theta)$$
$$\implies \sin \theta=0\implies\theta=\begin{cases}
0 & \text{Ferromagnets} \\
180 & \text{Anti-Ferromagnets}
\end{cases}$$
or
$$\cos \theta=-\frac{J_{1}}{4J_{2}}$$
for helimagnetism.
This creates three phases in $J$ space
