The most basic unit in magnetism is the magnetic moment, $\underline{\mu}$.
Consider an electron in a circular orbit.
![[Pasted image 20250122161459.png]]
The magnetic moment is define by
$$\underline{\mu}=I\underline{A}$$The magnetic moment defines the poles of a magnet
![[Pasted image 20250122161845.png]]

The orbital angular momentum is given by $\underline{L}=\underline{r}\times \underline{p}=\underline{r}\times m\underline{v}$.
$\underline{\mu}$ and $\underline{L}$ are antiparallel for negatively charged particles.
$$\therefore \underline{\mu}=-\gamma \underline{L}$$
where $\gamma$ is the gyroscopic ratio.
This shows magnetism and angular momentum are linked.
![[Pasted image 20250122162158.png]]
Consider an iron bar suspended, if an external $B$ field is applied, the magnetic moment means there is angular momentum in the system. As the was no L initially, there must be an induced angular momentum to counteract this. This also proves magnetism and angular momentum are linked.

To work out the gyroscopic ratio, consider the period of the electron's orbit
$$T=\frac{2\pi r}{V}\implies I=\frac{e}{T}=\frac{ev}{2\pi r}$$
$$|\underline{\mu}|=I|\underline{A}|=I\pi r^2=\frac{evr}{2}.$$
$$|\underline{L}|=|\underline{r}\times m\underline{v}|=mvr$$
since $r$ is orthogonal to $v$.
$$|\underline{\mu}|=\gamma mvr=\frac{evr}{2}$$
$$\implies \gamma=\frac{e}{2m}.$$
The Bohr magneton is the magnetic dipole per electron.
$$\mu_{B}=\gamma |\underline{L}|.$$
In a solid, $\underline{B}=\mu_{0}(\underline{H}+\underline{M})$ where $\underline{M}$ is the magnetisation (net magnetic moment per unit volume).
Weak magnetic fields and weak magnetic materials:
$$\underline{M}=\chi \underline{H}$$
where $\chi$ is the volume magnetic susceptibility.
$$\underline{B}=\mu_{0}(1+\chi)\underline{H}.$$

The torque of this procession is
$$\underline{\tau}=\underline{\mu} \times \underline{B}$$
$$\underline{\tau}=\frac{d\underline{L}}{dt};\;\underline{\mu}=-\gamma \underline{L}$$
$$\therefore \frac{d\underline{\mu}}{dt}=-\gamma(\underline{\mu} \times \underline{B}).$$
so $\underline{\mu}$ processes around the B field.
This shows classical physics predicts that magnetisation should be zero.
This means we need a quantum mechanical description to explain magnetism.
