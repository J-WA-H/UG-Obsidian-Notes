[[Ferromagnetism]] is very rare, only happens in transition metals iron, nickel, and cobalt with outer d orbitals.
Consider a zero B field material,
![[Pasted image 20250206154600.png]]
If an [[Exchange Interaction]] is introduced, it tries to align the spins so the electrons in $\delta E$ are transferred to the spin up band.
![[Pasted image 20250206154701.png]]
As there is no longer a balance of spins, this creates a molecular $B$ field which shifts the bands
![[Pasted image 20250206154748.png]]
With the exchange energy there is nothing stopping this process from happening until all spins align, but as this happens kinetic energy increases so these two energies must balance.

There are two energy contributions:
- Decrease in magnetic potential energy
- Increase in electron kinetic energy

$$\Delta E_{KE}=\left[ \frac{1}{2}g(E_{F})\delta E \right]\delta E.$$
The bracket gives the number of electrons transferred.
$$\Delta E_{KE}=\frac{1}{2}g(E_{F})\delta E^2.$$
For the potential energy,
$$M=\mu_{B}(\Delta n_{\uparrow}-\Delta n_{\downarrow})$$
$$\Delta n_{\uparrow}=\frac{1}{2}g(E_{F})\delta E;\;\Delta n_{\uparrow}=-\Delta n_{\downarrow}.$$
$$\therefore |M|=g(E_{F})\mu_{B}\delta E.$$
This magnetisation comes from the small change in the molecular $B$ field.
$$\Delta E_{PE}=-\int_{0}^{M}\underline{M}\cdot d\underline{B}_{mf}=-\lambda \int_{0}^{M}MdM=-\frac{\lambda M^2}{2}$$
$$\Delta E_{PE}=-\frac{1}{2}(\lambda \mu_{B}^2)[g(E_{F})\delta E]^2.$$
Define the Coulomb energy $U=\lambda \mu_{B}^2$.
The total energy is hence given by
$$\Delta E=\Delta E_{KE}+\Delta_{PE}=\frac{1}{2}g(E_{F})\delta E^2-\frac{1}{2}U[g(E_{F})\delta E]^2$$$$=\frac{1}{2}g(E_{F})\delta E^2[1-Ug(E_{F})].$$
$\Delta E>0$ for ferromagnetism to be stable
$$\implies Ug(E_{F})>1.$$
This is the Stoner criterion.

For ferromagnetism to be stable, we need a large $U$ and a large $g(E_F)$.
![[Pasted image 20250206160143.png]]
Hence why materials with outer d-orbitals are more likely to satisfy the Stoner criteria.
