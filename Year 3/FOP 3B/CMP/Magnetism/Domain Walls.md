Consider a [ferromagnetic](Ferromagnetism.md) solid:
![[Pasted image 20250206114835.png]]
Using the expression for the $B$ field, $\underline{B}=\mu_{0}(\underline{H}+\underline{M})$ and Maxwell II, $\underline{\nabla}\cdot \underline{B}=0$.
This gives
$$\underline{\nabla}\cdot \underline{M}=-\underline{\nabla}\cdot \underline{H}.$$
At the surface of the Ferromagnetic solid, there is a gradient in magnetisation as it drops to zero, which induces an opposite $\underline{H}$ field. This induced field is $\underline{H}_{d}$, the demagnetising field.
$$E=-\underline{M}\cdot(\mu_{0}\underline{H}_{d})>0.$$
Given the magnetisation points from the south pole to the north, we can split the solid into bar magnets.
![[Pasted image 20250206122704.png]]
In the right diagram there is a flow of magnetisation, and there is no gradient to zero $M$ at the surfaces.
These 4 zones are called domains, and the boundary between them is the domain wall.

There are two types of domain wall:
- Néel domain wall - spins rotate perpendicular to the domain wall plane
	![[Pasted image 20250206123017.png]]
- Bloch domain wall - spins rotate parallel to domain wall plane
	![[Pasted image 20250206123137.png]]
