Consider Larmor procession with an applied $B$ field in the positive $z$ direction:
![[Pasted image 20250123111156.png]]
The [[Magnetic Dipoles]] processes with radius $\rho$ and angular velocity
$$\omega=\gamma B=\frac{eB}{2m}.$$
This is the Larmor frequency.
Consider the top view:
![[Pasted image 20250123111408.png]]
Using the right hand rule to work out the direction of $\underline{A}$, and hence $\underline{\mu}$, it goes into the page, so it opposes the direction of the B field.
The period of the electron is 
$$T=\frac{2\pi}{\omega}=\frac{4\pi m}{eB}$$
$$\implies I=\frac{e}{T}=\frac{e^2B}{4\pi m}$$
$$\therefore |\underline{\mu}|=-\frac{e^{2}B}{4 m}\langle \rho^2\rangle.$$
For a spherical atom. $\langle x^2\rangle=\langle y^2\rangle=\langle z^\text{}\rangle$:
$$\langle r^2 \rangle =\langle x^2 \rangle+\langle y^2 \rangle  +\langle z^{2} \rangle =3\langle x^2 \rangle .$$
This gives for Larmor procession in the $xy$ plane:
$$\langle \rho^2 \rangle =\langle x^2 \rangle +\langle y^2 \rangle =\frac{2}{3}\langle x^2 \rangle .$$
$$\therefore |\underline{\mu}|=-\frac{e^2B}{6m}\langle r^2 \rangle. $$
