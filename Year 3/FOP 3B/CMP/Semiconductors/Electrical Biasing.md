For forward biasing:
![[Pasted image 20250220160357.png]]
A [[pn-Junctions]] is connected to cell. This cell induced an electric field which opposes the internal field of the pn junction. This brings down the net E-field. As there is a smaller E-field, the [potential](Potential%20of%20pn-Junctions.md) inside the pn junction decreases, $\phi_{bi}\to \phi_{bi}-|V_{ext}|$.
This decreases the width of the SCR.

For reverse biasing, the polarity of the cell is reversed.
![[Pasted image 20250220161515.png]]
In this case the induced electric field that is parallel to the internal field so the net E-field increases, $\phi_{bi}\to \phi_{bi}+|V_{ext}|$.
This increases the SCR width.

Considering the band structure of a pn junction,
![[Pasted image 20250220161920.png]]
The force induced by the internal electric field, $\underline{F}=-e\underline{E}_{bi}$ causes a diffusion of electrons, but the induced E-field opposes this motion.
To move from the n-side to the p-side, there is an energy barrier so few electrons diffuse past this boundary.
Moving from the p-side to the n-side is energetically favourable for electrons, but as there are so few in the p-side this is rare.

At equilibrium, $J_{n-side}=J_{p-side}\implies J_{net}=0$.

In forward biasing, this barrier is reduced so the diffusion from n to p is more likely.
![[Pasted image 20250220162405.png]]
This also splits the chemical potential.
As the diffusion is more likely, $J_{n-side}\gg J_{p-side}$ so there is a large current flow.

In reverse biasing, the barrier is increased making it harder for diffusion from the n-side to the p-side.
![[Pasted image 20250220162550.png]]
There is a only a small current from the p-side as $J_{n-side}\to 0$ so there is a small net current.

The diode equation is
$$J=J_{0}\left( \exp\left[ \frac{eV_{ext}}{k_{B}T} \right]-1 \right).$$
![[Pasted image 20250220162736.png]]
This different from Ohm's law for a p-type or n-type semiconductor which gives a linear current density.

This shows the behaviour of the current of a pn junction under biasing, with reverse biasing having a small current $J=-J_{0}$ and forward biasing having a large current.

This can be applied to devices, for example a light emitting diode (LED).
![[Pasted image 20250220163258.png]]
On the p-side there are many holes but few electrons, and the n-side has many electrons and few holes.

Applying forward bias to reduce the barrier, electrons flow into the p-side.
Recombination happens where the minority carriers combine with the majority carriers to keep the system in equilibrium - which emits photons as the electrons drop energy levels. For example, when holes are injected to the left side by the circuit, they are majority carriers as there are more holes than electrons, but as they move across the SCR, they combine with the electrons that become the majority carriers so the holes become minority carriers.

pn junctions are also used in solar cells.
![[Pasted image 20250220164248.png]]
In solar cells light is converted to electricity. When photons with energy greater than the band gap is absorbed, electron-hole pairs are generated by promoting VB electrons into the CB. The electrons and holes diffuse randomly through the material and therefore there is no net current. The built in electric field of the pn junction is what generates the current. Provided the photon generated electrons from the p-layer diffuse to the SCR they can be injected into the n-layer through the electric field, similarly holes from the n-layer can be injected into the p-layer. The electrons and holes are extracted by the electric field flow through the external circuit as an electric current.
