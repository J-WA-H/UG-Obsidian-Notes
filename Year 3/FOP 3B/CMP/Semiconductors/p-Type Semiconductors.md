Consider a boron atom in the middle of an [[Intrinsic Semiconductors]] lattice of Si,
![[Pasted image 20250219170514.png]]
Boron has 3 valence electrons so there is an electron missing to form the final covalent bond. As $T>0\text{ K}$, an electron is transferred from one of the nearby covalent bonds to the boron atom.
This creates a hole in the valence band.
![[Pasted image 20250219170704.png]]
This ionises B. This implies that the acceptors are ionised, and holes in the valence band are generated. There are no electrons in the conduction band.

This is a p-type semiconductor.

![[Pasted image 20250219170855.png]]
The acceptor electron must be in its own energy level at it cannot be in the valance band as it is in an ionised boron atom and it cannot be in the conduction band as it cannot move freely through the crystal.

The temperature dependence is similar to that for [[n-Type Semiconductors]],
![[Pasted image 20250219171106.png]]
As the valence band is full but the acceptor and covalent band aren't so the chemical potential must lie between $E_{V}$ and $E_{a}$.
![[Pasted image 20250219171235.png]]
In the saturation regime (room temperature) the chemical potential is given by
$$\mu=E_{V}+k_{B}T\ln\left( \frac{N_{V}}{N_{A}} \right)$$where $N_A$ is the acceptor concentration.
