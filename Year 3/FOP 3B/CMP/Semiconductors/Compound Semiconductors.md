Compound semiconductors have two elements for different [fcc sub lattices](Crystal%20Structure.md). 
To consider the optoelectronic properties of the [band gap](Band%20Gaps%20in%20Semiconductors.md) - its interaction with light - the different types of band gap.

The direct band gap has $E_{C},E_{V}$ at the same $k$-point, e.g. GaAs.
![[Pasted image 20250212162150.png]]
The indirect band has a $k$ point offset, e.g. Si.
![[Pasted image 20250212162230.png]]

Consider an incoming photon with $h\nu= E_{g}$. An electron at the top of the VB gets excited into the CB, at the same $k$ point as photons have little momentum. This shows that light absorption is good.
![[Pasted image 20250212162507.png]]

For indirect semiconductors, when $h\nu=E_{g}$, there is no electron state at $k=0$ so it needs to gain momentum. Phonons have little energy but high momentum so this gives the electron momentum without increasing its energy. As a photon and phonon needs to be absorbed, light absorption is bad.
![[Pasted image 20250212162800.png]]
Consider an alloy $A_{x}B_{1-x}$ where $A,B$ can represent an element of compound and $x$ is the relative abundance, $0\leq x\leq 1$.
The band gap is given by Vegard's law:
$$E_{g,A,B}=xE_{g,A}+(1-x)E_{g,B}-bx(1-x).$$
$b$ is the Bowing parameter.

The lattice parameter of this alloy is given by
$$a_{AB}=xa_{A}+(1-x)a_{B}.$$
