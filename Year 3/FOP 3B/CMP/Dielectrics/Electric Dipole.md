Consider a capacitor,
![[Pasted image 20250305160650.png]]
The capacitance of the dielectric is given by
$$C=\frac{Q}{V}=\varepsilon_{0}\varepsilon_{r}\left( \frac{A}{d} \right).$$
When a dielectric material is placed between a dielectric, $\varepsilon_{r}>1$ so capacitance increases and as the charge must remain constant, the potential difference decreases.
This polarises the material.

Definition: A dielectric is an insulator that can be polarised in an electric field.

Analogous to [[Magnetic Dipoles]], there is an electric dipole moment caused by two opposite charges placed at a distance $d$ from each other,
![[Pasted image 20250305161543.png]]
The magnitude of the dipole moment is given by
$$|\underline{\mu}|=qd.$$
In an electric field the energy is given by
$$E=-\underline{\mu}\cdot \underline{E}$$
and the torque is
$$\tau=\underline{\mu}\times \underline{B}.$$
There are examples of dipoles that appear in nature.
e.g. a neutral atom:
The atom has a positively charged nucleus with a negatively charged electron cloud around it.
![[Pasted image 20250305161818.png]]
As the positive and negative charge clouds overlap, there is no separation between them so there is no dipole moment in the atom.

If an E-field is applied, this gives a force of $\underline{F}=q\underline{E}=-e\underline{E}$ so it pushes the electron cloud in the opposite direction to the E-field. The proton isn't pushed so it doesn't move. 
![[Pasted image 20250305162045.png]]
This produces a dipole moment that is induced by the electric field.

Example: Water Molecule
![[Pasted image 20250305162311.png]]
Electrons are slightly attracted to the oxygen atom so there is a dipole aligned with each bond, creating a net dipole moment $\underline{\mu}_{net}$.
