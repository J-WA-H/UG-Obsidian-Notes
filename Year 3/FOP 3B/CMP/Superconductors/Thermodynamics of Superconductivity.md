Consider a [type I](Type%20I%20and%20II%20Superconductors.md) superconductor.
![[Pasted image 20250227123708.png]]
The energy of a [magnet moment](Magnetic%20Dipoles.md) is
$$E=-\underline{\mu}\cdot \underline{B}.$$
Per unit volume it is
$$\varepsilon =-\underline{M}\cdot \underline{B}.$$
For a superconductor, consider applying a B-field below the critical temperature. The Gibbs' free energy is given by
$$G_{S}[B]-G_{S}[0]=\int_{0}^{B}-\underline{M}\cdot d\underline{B}=\int_{0}^{B} \frac{BdB}{\mu_{0}}=\frac{B^2}{2\mu_{9}}.$$
This uses the fact that $B=0$ so $M=-H=-\frac{B}{\mu_{0}}$.
This gives the free energy as a function of the critical B-field,
$$G_{S}[B_{c}(T)]-G_{S}[0]=\frac{B_{c}^2(T)}{2\mu_{0}}.$$
This shows that the free energy of a superconductor is a quadratic in magnetic field.

In the normal state, $M\approx 0$ so
$$G_{N}[B_{c}(T)]=G_{N}[0].$$
Free energy has no dependence on the B-field.
![[Pasted image 20250227144237.png]]
At the phase boundary, $G_{S}[B_{c}(T)]=G_{N}[B_{c}(T)]=G_{N}[0]$$$\therefore G_{N}[0]-G_{S}[0]=\frac{B_{c}^2(T)}{2\mu_{0}}.$$
This difference in free energy at zero B-field is the condensation energy.

For a supercurrent, this is of order $\mu eV/\text{atom}$.

At $T=T_{c}$, the critical B-field is zero so $G_{N}[0]=G_{S}[0]$.
This implies there is no latent heat = which means the transition from the normal to the superconducting state is a second order phases transition.

This also implies entropy is continuous,
![[Pasted image 20250227144651.png]]
Superconducting electrons are therefore more ordered than normal state electrons.

This gives a [London penetration length](Meissner%20Effect.md) of
$$\lambda_{L}=\sqrt{ \frac{m_{e}}{\mu_{0}n_{S}e^2} }$$
where $n_{s}$ is the superconducting electron density.

At temperature drops below $T_{c}$, $\Delta S$ increases so $n_{S}$ increases so $\lambda_{L}$ decreases.
