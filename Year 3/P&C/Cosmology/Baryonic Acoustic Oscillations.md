Baryons move with sound waves through the universe. When the sound wave stops travelling, baryons/photons have accumulated on rings of comoving size $145\text{ Mpc}$.

$145\text{ Mpc}$ is a characteristic length scale imprinted on the [[Cosmic Microwave Background]] map.

The same characteristic length is imprinted in the baryon distribution, hence galaxies tend to form at these distances.

The galaxy correlation function calculates the number of galaxies in a given separation.
$$\xi(r_{CM})=\frac{n(r_{CM})}{\bar{n}}-1$$
where $n(r_{CM})$ is the number of galaxies in the shell and $\bar{n}$ is the mean number density of the galaxies.

This has a peak at $r_{CM}=145\text{ Mpc}$. This is the BAO peak.

Advantages to using the BAO as a standard ruler is that unlike CMB photons, galaxies have various redshifts - and other objects can be observed to higher redshifts - such as quasars.
