Consider a source, $S$, in the centre of a sphere in Euclidean space, whit a detector at the south pole.
![[Pasted image 20250211143339.png]]
The energy emitted by the source is
$$L\Delta t=f4\pi r^2\Delta t.$$
$$\implies r=\sqrt{ \frac{L}{4\pi f} }.$$
This distance is the luminosity distance, which in Euclidean space is given by,
$$d_{L}=\sqrt{ \frac{L}{4\pi f} }.$$
As the universe is expanding, and not necessarily flat, this needs modifying with the [[Friedmann-Robertson-Walker Metric]]. 

The radius used should be $S_{k}(r_{CM})$ instead of $r$.
$$L\Delta t=4\pi S_{k}^2(r_{CM})f\Delta t.$$
To modify $\Delta t$, time dilation needs to be considered.
![[Pasted image 20250211145109.png]]
$$\Delta t_{e}=\frac{\lambda_{e}}{c}$$
$$\Delta t_{0}=\frac{(1+z)\lambda_{e}}{c}=(1+z)\Delta t_{e}.$$
$$\therefore L\Delta t_{e}=4\pi S_{k}^2(r_{CM})\Delta t_{0}=4\pi S_{k}^2(r_{CM})(1+z)\Delta t_{e}.$$
For equality to hold, the LHS needs to be divided by $1+z$.
$$\frac{L\Delta t_{e}}{1+z}=4\pi S_{k}^2(r_{CM})f(1+z)\Delta t_{e}.$$
$$\implies d_{L}=\sqrt{ \frac{L}{4\pi f} }=(1+z)S_{k}(r_{CM}).$$
$$d_{L}=(1+{z})S_{k}(r_{CM}).$$
This gives a theoretical model for distance that depends on cosmological model.

Standard candles can be used to calibrate distances. 

A standard candle is an object with known luminosity. Type Ia supernovae are the most useful standard candle. They are formed by a white dwarf accreting matter from its binary companion - a giant star.
When the mass exceeds the Chandrasekhar mass, $1.44M_{\odot}$ then the self-gravity is too strong for electron degeneracy pressure to balance, causing a collapse, triggering nuclear fusion reactions. The energy released causes an explosion - a supernova.

All observed luminosities have the same light-curves, making them good standard candles.
