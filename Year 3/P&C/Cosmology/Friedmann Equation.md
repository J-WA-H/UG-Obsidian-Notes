The Friedmann equation gives a functional form for the [[Cosmic Scale Factor]]. It can be derived from Newtonian gravity.
Consider a sphere of uniform density.
![[Pasted image 20250121151418.png]]
From Newtonian gravity, 
$$\ddot{r}=-\frac{GM}{r^2}$$
$$\dot{r}\ddot{r}=-\frac{GM}{r^2}\dot{r}$$
integrate w.r.t time
$$\int \dot{r}\ddot{r}dt=-\int \frac{GM}{r^2}\dot{r}dt$$
$$\int \frac{d}{dt}\left( \frac{1}{2}\dot{r}^2 \right)dt=\int \frac{d}{dt}\left( \frac{GM}{r} \right)$$
$$\implies \frac{1}{2}\dot{r}^2=\frac{GM}{r}+c'$$
$$\dot{r}^2=\frac{2GM}{r}+c=\frac{2G}{r(t)}\left( \frac 43 \pi r^3(t)\rho(t) \right)+c$$
$$=\frac{8\pi G}{3}\rho(t)r^2(t)+c.$$
Using $r(t)=a(t)r_{0}$,
$$\left( \frac{\dot{a}}{a} \right)^2=\frac{8\pi G}{3}\rho -\frac{kc^2}{a^2}$$
