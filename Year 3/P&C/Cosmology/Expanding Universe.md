The Olbers' paradox proves the universe is finite in time. If the universe was infinite, then every line of sight will intersect a star somewhere - which would make the night sky as bright as the sun.
![[Pasted image 20250116112957.png]]
Assuming a uniform density of stars, $n$, the number of stars in the shell is given by
$$N=n{4}\pi r^{2}\Delta r.$$
The flux received from each star is 
$$f=\frac{L}{4\pi r^2}$$
so the total flux observed is
$$F=Nf=Ln\Delta r.$$
For $\Delta r \to 0$, we can integrate to find the total flux of the universe
$$F_{tot}=\int_{0}^{\infty}fNdr.$$
This integral doesn't converge which implies an infinite flux - which is contrary to observational evidence. 
The assumptions underlying the paradox are
- The universe is infinite in time
- Universe is infinite in space
- Universe is static
- Universe is [homogeneous](Overview%20of%20the%20Universe.md)
- Space is Euclidean.

This produces the modern view that the universe is infinite in space but finite in time.
