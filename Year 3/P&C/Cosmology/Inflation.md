All 3 [[Problems of Hot Big Bang]] model can be solved by inflation - a period of very rapid expansion before the onset of the radiation era.

The origin of this expansion is unknown, but the current model is that is is driven by exotic matter called the inflaton with density $\sim 10^{108}\text{ GeV/c}^2\text{ m}^{-3}$. It is thought to have started at the Planck time, $10^{-43}\text{ s}$ and ends at time $10^{-34}\text{ s}$.

The density of the universe is thought to have been constant so
$$a(t)\propto \exp[Ht].$$
The horizon problem is that all points in the CMB have the same temperature - which breaks causality.
Using the physical horizon size,
$$l_{H}=a(t)\int_{0}^{t} \frac{cdt'}{a(t')}.$$
In the radiation dominated era, $a(t)\propto t^{1/2}$,
$$l_{H}=t^{1/2}\int_{0}^{t} \frac{cdt'}{t'^{1/2}}=2ct.$$
In the matter dominated era, $a(t)\propto t^{2/3}$,
$$l_{H}=t^{2/3}\int_{0}^{t} \frac{cdt'}{t'^{2/3}}=3ct.$$
For inflation, $a(t)\propto \exp[Ht]$,
$$l_{H}=e^{Ht_{f}}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{f}} \frac{cdt'}{e^{Ht'}}=ce^{Ht_{f}} \frac{1}{H}[e^{-Ht_{i}}-e^{-Ht_{f}}]=\frac{c}{H}(e^{H(t_{f}-t_{i})}-\cancelto{0}{1})$$
$$l_{H}=\frac{c}{H}e^{ H(t_{f}-t_{f}) }.$$
During inflation, $H=10^{36} \text{ s}^{-1}$ so 
$$l_{H}=\frac{c}{H}e^{10^{36}(10^{-34}-10^{-43})}\approx \frac{c}{H}e^{100}.$$
This gives a large value of of $l_{H}$ which solves the horizon problem.

The larger sphere is the horizon of a point on the CMB, which encloses the whole observable universe so the whole CMB having the same temperature doesn't break causality with inflation.

Inflation also solves the flatness problem.
$$|\Omega_{k}(t)|=\frac{|k|c^2}{\dot{a}^2}.$$
Today, $\Omega_{k_{0}}=0.01$ and at the Planck time, $\Omega_{k}(10^{-43})=10^{-61}$. This requires a severe fine tuning of the initial condition of the universe, and is very unnatural. This is against the principle of naturalness and simplicity of the law of physics.

To fix this, find the ratio of $\Omega_{k}$ at the beginning and end of inflation.
$$\frac{|\Omega_{k}(t_{f})|}{|\Omega_{k}(t_{i})|}=\left( \frac{\dot{a}(t_{i})}{\dot{a}(t_{f})} \right)^2.$$
During inflation, $\dot{a}\propto H\exp[Ht]$
$$=\left( \frac{\exp[Ht_{i}]}{\exp[Ht_{f}]} \right)^2=e^{ -2H(t_{f}-t_{i}) }\approx e^{200}\sim 10^{-87}.$$
This shows that $\Omega_{k}$ has been greatly reduced during inflation. If the universe was initially curved, the rapid expansion during inflation flattens it out to the value measured today.

This also solves the relic abundance problem, as the rapid acceleration dilutes the abundance to levels that we cannot observe today. The abundance of GUT particles goes to zero during inflation then discontinuously jumps to the predicted value at the end of inflation.

Inflation is driven by inflatons. It's density remains constant during inflation and afterwards it decays into standard model particles, which is why we can't observe them.
