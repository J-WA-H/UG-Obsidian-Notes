Consider a light emitted at time $t_e$ and observed at time $t_{0}$. The [[Redshift]] of the source is given by
$$z=\frac{1}{a(t_{e})}-1.$$
![[Pasted image 20250203180103.png]]

The photon has to travel an increased distance due to the expansion of the universe.

The [time elapsed](Expansion%20History%20of%20the%20Universe.md) between two events, dependent on cosmological parameters, is given by
$$t(1\to 2)=\frac{1}{H_{0}}\int_{a_{1}}^{a_{2}} \frac{da}{\sqrt{ \Omega_{R_{0}}a^{-2}+\Omega_{M_{0}}a^{-1}+\Omega_{\Lambda_{0}}a^{2} }}.$$
As photons travel at the speed of light, the distance travelled is given by
$$d=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}cdt=\frac{c}{H_{0}}\int_{a_{1}}^{a_{2}} \frac{da}{\sqrt{ \Omega_{R_{0}}a^{-2}+\Omega_{M_{0}}a^{-1}+\Omega_{\Lambda_{0}}a^{2} }}.$$
The lookback time $t_{LB}$ is the time between an event taking place to now,
$$ct_{LB}(a_{1})=\frac{c}{H_{0}}\int_{a_{1}}^{a_{2}=1} \frac{da}{\sqrt{ \Omega_{R_{0}}a^{-2}+\Omega_{M_{0}}a^{-1}+\Omega_{\Lambda_{0}}a^{2} }}.$$
