Consider $N=nL^3$ relativistic particles moving randomly in a box. Relativistic particles are called radiation in cosmology. They have large random motions.

By symmetry, there should be an equal number of particles moving each of the $x,y,z$ directions.
$V=L^3$, number density $n\therefore N=nL^3$.
![[Pasted image 20250122150832.png]]
Each particle has momentum $p$ and energy $E=pc$, moving at $c$.

The number of particles moving in the $x$ direction is expected to be $\frac{N}{3}$.
The number of particles hitting the right wall should be $\frac{N}{6}$ in $\Delta t=\frac{L}{c}$.
These are elastic collisions s0 the total momentum received by the wall is $\Delta p=\frac{N}{6}\cdot{2}p=\frac{Np}{3}.$

The average force exerted is
$$F=\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}=\frac{Np/3}{L/c}=\frac{Npc}{3L}.$$
The pressure exerted is
$$P=\frac{F}{A}=\frac{Npc}{3L^3}=\frac{NE}{3V}=\frac{\rho c^2}{3}.$$
Even though there is no wall in the universe, the collisions of particles acts as a wall and as there are infinitely many particles, statistically, each particle will collide with another at some point. Even if there is no collision, it is mathematically equivalent as a collision

Non-relativistic particles are called matter in cosmology. They have negligible random motion. so $P_{M}=0$.
