The [expansion](Expanding%20Universe.md) of the universe stretches the wavelength of photons, $\lambda(t)=a(t)\lambda_{0}$, where $\lambda_{0}$ is the present wavelength.
The relationship between the present wavelength and its emitted wavelength describes cosmological redshift
$$\frac{\lambda_{0}}{\lambda_{e}}=1+z=\frac{a(t_{0})}{a(t_{e})}=\frac{1}{a(t_{e})}$$
where, by definition of the [[Cosmic Scale Factor]], $a(t_{0})=1$, and $t_{e}$ is the time the photon was emitted.
Cosmological redshift is given by
$$z=\frac{1}{a}-1.$$
As $t\to 0$, $a\to 0$ so $z\to \infty$.
As $t\to t_{0}$, $a\to 1$ so $z\to 0$.

At large scales, the redshift is dominated by [[Hubble Flow]], $z_{c}$, and at small scales, it is dominated by the standard doppler shift, $z_{D}$.
$$1+z_D=1+\frac{v}{c}$$
$$1+z_{c}=\frac{1}{a}.$$
$$1+z_{tot}=(1+z_{D})(1+z_{c})=\frac{1}{a}\left( 1+\frac{v}{c} \right).$$
