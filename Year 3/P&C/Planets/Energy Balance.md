Most of the energy supplied to a planet is from its host star. The sun has a spectrum described by a blackbody with temperature $T=5777\text{ K}$. Wien's displacement law gives a peak brightness at wavelength $\lambda=0.29/T\approx 500\text{ nm}$ for the sun. 
Planets have much smaller temperatures so mostly emit in the IR region.

Planets have reached a steady state so the power in equals the power out. The incoming power predominantly comes from the sun - a blackbody with a peak in the optical region.
Some of this incoming radiation is reflected of the planet back into space. The albedo, $A$, gives the fraction of incident flux that is reflected. 
Planets emit a temperature, $T_e$ satisfying
$$\frac{L_{\odot}}{4\pi r^2}\pi R_{p}^2(1-A)=\epsilon\sigma T_{e}^44\pi R_{p}^2.$$
$\epsilon=0.9-1$, if not given assume $\epsilon=1$.
This yields an effective temperature of
$$T_{e}=\left( \frac{L_{\odot} (1-A)}{16\pi r^2\epsilon\sigma_{4}} \right)^{1/4}.$$
