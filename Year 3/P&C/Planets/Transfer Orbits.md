A Hohmann transfer orbit is used to move between circular orbits of different radii. ![[Pasted image 20241025102747.png]]
The original orbit at $A$ has speed from the [Vis-viva equation](Velocities%20in%20orbit.md):
$$v_{1}^2=\mu\left( \frac{2}{a_{1}}-\frac{1}{a_{1}} \right)$$
The transfer orbit between A and B has a semi major axis
$$a_{t}=\frac{{a_{1}+a_{2}}}{2}.$$
To enter the transfer orbit, we need a speed of
$$v_{2}^2=\mu\left( \frac{2}{a_{1}}-\frac{1}{a_{t}} \right).$$
When an object has reached point B on its transfer orbit it has a speed of
$$v_{3}^2=\mu\left( \frac{2}{a_{2}}-\frac{1}{a_{t}} \right)$$

and to enter the final orbit the speed needed is
$$v_{4}^2=\mu\left( \frac{2}{a_{2}}-\frac{1}{a_{2}} \right).$$
The time taken for this transfer orbit to take place is half the time period of an orbit with semi major axis $a_{t}$,
$$\frac{T}{2}=\pi\left( \frac{a_{t}^3}{\mu} \right)^{1/2}.$$
Gravity assists can also be used to accelerate a spacecraft by flying by from behind a planet in its orbit. In the reference frame of the planet the spacecraft follows a hyperbolic trajectory and the outbound velocity has the same magnitude as the inbound velocity. In the reference frame of the sun, the spacecraft gains angular momentum at the expense of the planet and accelerate. Flying by in front of planets can decelerate a spacecraft.
