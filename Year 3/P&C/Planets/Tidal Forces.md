When two extended bodies orbit each other, the sides facing each other experience a stronger gravitational attraction than the far sides. Each body experiences a differential tidal force, acting to stretch it along the line connecting the two bodies.

The strength of the tidal force is given by
$$\frac{d}{dr}\left( \frac{Gm_{1}m_{2}}{r^2} \right)=-\frac{2Gm_{1}m_{2}}{r^3}$$
This shows the mass of tidal bulge is $m_{b}\propto{1}/r^3$.

A small body passing too close to a large body can be torn apart by the larger bodies tidal force. The **Roche limit** is the minimum separation before tidal destruction.
$$\delta F=\frac{2GMm}{r_{R}^3}\delta r=\frac{Gm^2}{(\delta r)^2}$$
using densities $m=\frac{4}{3}\pi\left( \frac{\delta r}{2} \right)^2\rho_{m}$ and $M=\frac{4}{3}\pi R^3\rho_{M}$ leads to
$$r_{R}=(16)^{1/3}\left( \frac{\rho_{M}}{\rho_{m}} \right)^{1/3}R.$$
