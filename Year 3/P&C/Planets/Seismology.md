Seismic waves are elastic waves that travel through the Earth. These waves travel at the local speed of sound - dependent on material density. The waves follow Fermat's principle in which they take the path that takes the least time

Seismographs are used to measure seismic waves - where the $y$ axis shows the displacement of the ground and the $x$ axis shows time.

As the figure shows, from a source, P-waves arrive first, then S-waves, then surface waves.

P-waves (primary wave) are longitudinal waves that travel through the Earth

S-waves (secondary waves) are transverse waves that travel through the Earth. These can have either vertical (SV) or horizontal (SH) polarisation.

The waves that cause the most damage to human infrastructure are SH waves and Rayleigh waves (combination of P and SV waves).
