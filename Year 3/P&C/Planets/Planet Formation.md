The requirements for planet formation are prograde, low ellipticity, low inclination, well spaced planetary orbit and mostly prograde spins. 

98% of the angular momentum in the solar system is from the planets, but 99.8% of the mass is in the sun.
The age of the solar system is $4.568\text{ Gyr}$.
Dichotomy between dense, small rocky terrestrial planets with low density gas and ice giants further from the sun.

Other features needing explanations are shapes, densities, locations, and orbit of populations of small bodies in the solar system, the abundance of orbits and compositions of moons, the presence of rings around all giant planets.

Solar nebula theory:
This theory suggests planets form in nebula during star formation, in molecular clouds containing mostly hydrogen and helium gas with some dust. Fragmentation occurs on scales of the Jean's mass. The star sized over dense cores collapse on a free fall timescale.
Angular momentum will be conserved, leading to a protoplanetary disc.
Stars form in the dense centre and angular momentum is transferred outwards via disc velocity or T Tauri winds.
Coplanar prograde planetary orbits result naturally.

Protoplanetary discs have three main evolution periods, in fall, internal evolution, clearing.

Planets must form from this disc before the third phase.

Giant Planet formation:
The frost line is the growth of structures within the protoplanetary disc is determined by which materials can condense at which temperature/heliocentric distances.

Planetary embryos:
Micron sizer grains condense out of protoplanetary nebula. Iron compounds and silicates in the inner regions of the disc with ice further out. Grain collisions lead to particles held together by Van-de-Waal forces.
These grains rapidly grow to form planetesimals at which point gravity becomes important.
Gravitational focussing by planetesimals and large impacts with other planetesimals produce well spaced planetary embryos.
