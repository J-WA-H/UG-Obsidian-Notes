From electromagnetism we know a magnetic field is proportional to a dipole moment.
We can quantify the dipole moment by
1. Current loop that produces such a field, we have dipole moment $m$ in $\text{A m}^{2}$.
2. The volume over which the field has a given strength gives $M$ in $\text{T m}^{2}$.
$$M=\frac{\mu_{0}}{4\pi}m.$$
Consider the $B$ field from a dipole centred on $r=0$.
$$B_{r}=\frac{2M}{r^3}\cos \theta;\;B_{\theta}=\frac{M}{r^3}\sin \theta.$$
To an order of magnitude, the dipole $B$ field is $B\approx \frac{M}{r^3}$.

Types of planetary magnetic fields:
1. Global dipolar fields
2. Global non-dipolar fields
3. Small fields arising from crustal magnetism

Origins of magnetic fields:
Primordial $B$ fields, like the Earth, could arise from
1. Ferromagnetic material in terrestrial planets becoming permanently magnetised.
2. Field trapped in conducting core during formation unable to escape for the lifetime of the solar system.

Evolution of $B$ fields:
Current density can de defined as
$$\underline{j}=\sigma(\underline{E}+\underline{u}\times \underline{B})$$
where $\sigma$ is the conductivity. For $\lvert \underline{u} \rvert\ll c$ we can ignore displacement current so Maxwell's equations become
$$\underline{\nabla}\cdot \underline{B}=0$$
$$\underline{\nabla}\times \underline{E}=-\frac{\partial \underline{B}}{\partial t}$$
$$\underline{\nabla}\times \underline{B}=\mu\underline{j}=\mu\sigma(\underline{u}\times \underline{B}).$$
This yields a wave equation for $B$:
$$\frac{\partial \underline{B}}{\partial t}=\underline{\nabla}\times(\underline{u}\times \underline{B})+\frac{1}{\mu\sigma}\nabla^2\underline{B}.$$
Consider two cases:
1. Fluid is at rest
	$$\frac{\partial \underline{B}}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{\sigma \mu}\nabla^2\underline{B}\text{  (diffusion equation)}.$$
2. Fluid in motion and $\sigma\to \infty$:
	$$\frac{\partial \underline{B}}{\partial t}=\underline{\nabla}\times(\underline{u}\times \underline{B}).$$
The $B$ field is carried along by the fluid - it is frozen in. Generally, $B$ fields will do a combination of both.
Magnetic Reynolds number:
$$R_{m}=uL\sigma \mu.$$
Low $R_m$ is where diffusion dominates over convection.

Earth's $B$ field needs constant regeneration.
