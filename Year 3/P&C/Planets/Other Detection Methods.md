Direct imaging is another method used to detect exoplanets however it is very limited as the light from the planet is normally dwarfed by the light from the star.

The ratio of the planet/stellar brightness depends on the size and wavelength dependent [albedo](Energy%20Balance.md), $P(\lambda,\alpha)$.
$$\frac{L_{p}}{L_{\odot}}=P(\lambda,\alpha)\left( \frac{R_{p}}{R_{*}} \right)^2.$$
Another method is using timings for pulsar planets. Core collapse supernovae can produce rapidly spinning neutron stars that emit radio pulses. These provide really accurate clocks with spin-down rates of $\approx 10^{-11}$.

For circular, edge-on orbits, and assuming a canonical pulsar mass of $1.35\text{ M}_{\odot}$, the amplitude of timing residuals is
$$\tau_{p}=1.2\left( \frac{M_{p}}{M_{\odot}} \right)\left( \frac{T}{1 \text{ year}} \right)^{2/3}\text{ ms}.$$
Gravitational microlensing is another method. The focusing of light rays from a distant source by an intervening mass. We need a background star aligned behind a lensing star exoplanet system. Events are characterised by the Einstein ring radius,
$$R_{E}=\left[ \frac{GM_{L}}{c^2} \frac{(D_{S}-D_{L})D_{L}}{D_{S}}\right]^{1/2}=8.1\left( \frac{M_{L}}{M_{\odot}} \right)^{1/2}\left( \frac{D_{S}}{8\text{ kpc}} \right)^{1/2}[(1-d)d]^{1/2}\text{ Au}$$
where $M_L$ is the mass of the lensing object, $D_{L}$ and $D_{S}$ are the lens and source distances, and $d=\frac{D_{L}}{D_{S}}$.
In angular coordinates, the Einstein ring radius is
$$\theta_{E}=\frac{R_{E}}{D_{L}}=1.0\left( \frac{M_{L}}{M_{\odot}} \right)^{1/2}\left( \frac{D_{L}}{8\text{ kpc}} \right)^{-1/2}(1-d)^{1/2}\text{ mas}.$$
Microlensing magnification varies with time as the lensing star and planet pass in front of the source. Defining $v=200v_{200}\text{ km s}^{-1}$, the timescale of the event is
$$t_{E}=69.9\left( \frac{M_{L}}{M_{\odot}} \right)^{1/2}\left( \frac{D_{S}}{8 \text{ kpc}} \right)^{1/2}[(1-d)d]^{1/2}v_{200}^{-1}\text{ days}.$$
Probability of sufficiently aligned lens is very small, $\approx 10^{-6}$ for the source stars in the galactic bulge, Magellanic cloud or M31, even if all galactic DM is in lensing object. Lensing events are achromatic, unlike much stellar variability.
We can find Earth sized exoplanets but only once and systems are often too faint and distant to follow up with other techniques.
