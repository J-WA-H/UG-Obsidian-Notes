Convection is heat transfer by molecular collisions. It is important within solids, at the boundary between the surface and the atmosphere, and in the upper atmosphere where molecules have long mean-free paths due to small number of paths.

Convection is heat transfer by bulk-fluid motion. It is important in planets with fluid interiors and in dense atmospheres.

Consider a parcel of fluid that is hotter than surrounding fluid. It is overpressured, so will expand, become less dense and rise, cooling as it does so. This continues until the temperature difference disappears, or a material boundary is met.  
Efficiency depends upon the temperature gradient in the fluid.

The adiabatic lapse rate ,$\Gamma$, is the temperature gradient present after convection has redistributed heat in an ideal gas atmosphere.
$$\Gamma=-\frac{dT}{dr}.$$
Heat expansion is so fast that $dq=0$, so this is an adiabatic process.

For ideal gas, $TP^{(1-\gamma)/\gamma}=\text{constant}$, $\gamma=\frac{C_{P}}{C_{v}}$.
Using the chain rule:
$$\Gamma=-\frac{dT}{dP} \frac{dP}{dr}.$$
Using the ideal gas expression and [[Hydrostatic Equilibrium]], we get
$$\Gamma=-\left( \frac{{\gamma-1}}{\gamma} \right) \frac{\mu m_{H}}{\rho k_{B}}(-\rho g)=\left( \frac{{\gamma-1}}{\gamma} \right) \frac{\mu m_{H}g}{k_{B}}.$$
