The habitable zone of a star is the range in sun-star separation where liquid water exists on the planet surface.

We also need to consider atmosphere, planet rotation, type of star and evolution. and the mass and composition of the planet.

We can relate the [effective temperature](Energy%20Balance.md) of a planet's surface to that of the star
$$L_{*}=4\pi R^{2}_{*}\sigma T^{4}_{*}$$
$$T_{e}=\left( \frac{L_{*}(1-A)}{16\pi\epsilon\sigma r^2} \right)^{1/4}\implies T_{e}=T_{*}\sqrt{ \frac{R_{*}}{2r} }(1-A)^{1/4}.$$
For $273<T_{e}<373\text{ K}$, we get the solar system habitable zone to be $0.47\text{ Au}<a<0.87\text{ Au}$.

Accounting for atmospheres and the [greenhouse effect](Radiation%20and%20the%20Greenhouse%20effect.md), the habitable zone becomes $0.95\text{ Au}<a<1.15\text{ Au}$.

For terrestrial planets, there is a balance between the greenhouse effect and the condensation of carbon dioxide.

The planet itself needs a mass $>0.5\text{ M}_{*}$ otherwise the planet will have lost internal heat.
We also need a mass $<10\text{ M}_{*}$ otherwise it is a giant.

The Drake equation gives the number of civilisations in our galaxy where communication is possible.
$$N=R_{*}f_{p}n_{e}f_{l}f_{i}f_{c}L$$
where $R_{*}$ is the star formation rate, $f_{p}$ is the fraction of stars hosting planets, $n_{e}$ is the average number of planets per star, $f_{l}$ is the fraction of planets that host life, $f_{i}$ is the fraction of planets with intelligent life, $f_{l}$ is the fraction of intelligent life that can emit detectable signals, and $L$ is the duration over which signals are emitted.
