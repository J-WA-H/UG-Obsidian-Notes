Radiation is the dominant heat transport mechanism for moderate density regions in the atmosphere.

Efficiency of radiation depends on molecules present and wavelengths. If flux is independent of altitude, then it is in radiative equilibrium.

The blackbody spectrum of the sun mostly passes through Earth's atmosphere to reach the ground. 
Reradiated IR radiation strongly absorbed by $CO_{2}$ and $H_{2}O$. Molecules reradiate energy in all directions - trapping in more heat.

To quantify the greenhouse effect, consider the atmosphere being split into layers of different temperatures which absorbs all radiation incident on it.
![[Pasted image 20241119185154.png]]
Considering the top layer, where $T_{1}=T_{e}$, the power in must equal the power out so $2\sigma T_{e}^4=\sigma T_{2}^4$.
For the next layer, $2\sigma T_{2}^4=\sigma T_{1}^4+\sigma T_{3}^4\implies T_{3}^4=3T_{e}^4$.
More generally, $T_m^4=mT_{e}^4$.
For the layer next to the ground:
$$2\sigma T_{n}^4=\sigma T_{g}^4+\sigma T_{n-1}^4.$$
This gives 
$$T_{g}^4=(n+1)T_{e}^4$$
where $n$ is the optical depth.
