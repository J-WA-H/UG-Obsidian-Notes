Consider 3 classes of ensembles all in thermal equilibrium - the averages of the physical observables are constant.
$$\frac{{d\langle A \rangle }}{dt}=0\implies \frac{d\rho}{dt}=0$$
from the [[Von-Neumann Equation]].

This implies that the Hamiltonian is time independent.
From the Von-Neumann equation $[H,\rho]=0$ so the Hamiltonian and the [[Density Operator]] commute so we can have a basis which makes both $H$ and $\rho$ diagonal.
$$\bra{n} H\ket{m} =H_{nm}=E_{n}\delta_{nm}.$$
$$\bra{n} \rho \ket{m} =\rho_{nm}=\rho_{nn}\delta_{nm}.$$
The probability of the subsystem from the ensemble is in the eigenstate $\ket{n}$ is $\rho_{nn}$.
We can express $\rho$ in terms of $H$, $\rho=\rho(t)$$\implies \rho_{nn}$ can be expressed in terms of $E_{n}$.
The different functional dependence of $\rho_{nn}$ on $E_n$ will define the different ensembles.
