The EPR paradox is:
Consider the decay of a $\pi^{0}$ meson with spin zero and is at rest at $t_{0}$. This decays into $e^{+}e^{ - }$.
This is a rare process so this is more of a thought experiment.
For spin 0, the spin wavefunction is
$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{ 2 }}(\ket{\uparrow\downarrow} -\ket{\downarrow\uparrow} ).$$
At some position $x_{e}$ at time $t_{e}>t_{0}$, we measure the spin of the electron. If we measure $\uparrow$ for the electron, the wavefunction collapses to $\ket{\uparrow\downarrow}$, so any future measurement of the positron gives $\downarrow$.

For $t_{0}<t<t_{e}$, $\frac{1}{\sqrt{ 2 }}(\ket{\uparrow\downarrow}-\ket{\downarrow\uparrow})$
For $t>t_{e}$, $\ket{\uparrow\downarrow}$.
The electron spin measurement alters the spin state of the positron over an arbitrarily large distance.
This is non-local.

In the realistic interpretation, at production the electron chooses a spin direction, the positron must have the opposite spin and there a hidden variable that chooses the spin. This is local.

Performing the experiment many times, you can't predict the spin of the electron but the positron is always opposite.
