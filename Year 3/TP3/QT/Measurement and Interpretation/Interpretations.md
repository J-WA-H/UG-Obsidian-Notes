Quantum mechanics is one of the most successful physical theories. There is amazing agreement between theory and experiment, so something about quantum mechanics is correct.

However, since the beginning there has been a lot of debate of the interpretation of quantum mechanics.
Many are due to the probabilistic nature of quantum mechanics, and the measurement process.
This is where the debate becomes philosophical. 

Those who work in the field usually shut up and calculate.

Quantum mechanics is inherently probabilistic. This is a major source of debate.
There are hidden variable theories which replace the inherent randomness with statistical randomness.

Start with the basics of quantum mechanics:
- Observables are described by Hermitian operators and a complete set of basis states
	$$\hat{A}\ket{n} =a_{n}\ket{n} $$
- States are described by a wavefunction $\ket{\psi}$ which can be written as a linear combination of basis states
	$$\ket{\psi}= \sum_{n}c_{n}\ket{n}. $$
- Time evolution is governed by the Hamiltonian
	$$i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial t}\ket{\psi} =\hat{H}\ket{\psi}. $$
- The probability of measuring $\ket{\psi}$ having a value $a_{n}$ is $|c_{n}|^2$.
- If we measure $A$ and get $a_n$, the wavefunction collapses to the state $\ket{n}$

Consider a particle at position $\underline{x}_{0}$ and at time $t_{0}$. 
At $t_{0}$ we measure position $x_0$. The wavefunction collapses to a position eigenstate $x$.

If we measure the position at some earlier time $t_{0}-\delta t$, it is either
- at point $\underline{x}_{0}$ - realistic interpretation
- no where - orthodox interpretation
- no answer - agnostic interpretation

If the particle is at $\underline{x}_{0}$ or $\underline{x}_{0}-vdt$, we cannot describe this and the current formalisation fails.

We need a more fundamental theory with all quantum mechanical results, but knows where the particle is before measurement. This is the hidden variable theory.
Extra parameters $\implies$ hidden variables
Inherent randomness $\to$ statistical randomness

There is a due ignorance as there is not a fundamental randomness.

The Copenhagen interpretation states that the particle doesn't have a position before measurement and the measurement forces the particle to choose a position via the collapse of a wavefunction.

The practical interpretation states that quantum mechanics cannot give an answer.

As we are physicists, a different theory must make testable predictions which are different from the current theory.
