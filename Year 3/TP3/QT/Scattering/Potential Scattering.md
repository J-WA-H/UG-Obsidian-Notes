The scattering of a particle of mass $m$ by a potential $V(\underline{r})$ is given by Schrodinger equation
$$i\hbar \frac{\partial \Psi(\underline{r},t)}{\partial t}=\left[ -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2+V(\underline{r}) \right]\Psi(\underline{r},t).$$
This also applies for two particles, where the mass moves to the reduced mass, $m\to \mu$.
Since the potential is independent of time so we can ansatz $\Psi(\underline{r},t)=\psi(\underline{r})e^{iEt/\hbar}$.
This gives the time independent Schrodinger equation
$$\left[ -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2+V(\underline{r}) \right]\psi(\underline{r})=E\psi(\underline{r}).$$
The energy is given by
$$E=\frac{\hbar^2k^2}{2m},$$
so plugging this into the Schrodinger equation gives
$$[\nabla^2+k^2-U(\underline{r})]\psi(\underline{r})=0.$$
$U(\underline{r})$ is the reduced potential, $U(\underline{r})=\frac{2m}{\hbar^2}V(\underline{r})$.
