Both the [Klein-Gordon equation](Interpreting%20Klein-Gordon%20Solutions.md) and the [Dirac equation](Plane%20Wave%20Solutions.md) show negative energy solutions.
Ultimately we need quantum field theory to solve these problems due to single particle wavefunctions. 

One theory is Dirac's hole theory. 
Consider two energy bands, like in a [semiconductor](Band%20Gaps%20in%20Semiconductors.md), where above the highest energy and below the lowest energy there is a continuum of states, with a band gap in between.
![[Pasted image 20250311152740.png]]
Dirac said all negative energy states are full - which works for fermions as the Pauli-exclusion principle means only 1 electron can occupy a state.
The transition from positive to negative energy states is forbidden.

In a vacuum, there is an infinite sea of electrons in negative energy states - there are no positive energy electrons.

Suppose a negative energy electron interacts with a photon of $E>mc^2$,
![[Pasted image 20250311153159.png]]
This is analogous to pair creation, a photon decays into an electron and positron (hole).
The inverse analogy is also true, the deexcitation is analogous to electron-positron annihilation.

With respect to a vacuum, the hole has a positive charge and energy. This says nothing about the interactions of the electrons in the sea.

Considering the sea, this is no longer a single particle equation.

This theory works for fermions but doesn't account for bosons and the Klein-Gordon equation.
