In the Lorenz gauge we require $\underline{\nabla}\cdot \underline{A}=-\mu_{0}\varepsilon_{0} \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial t}$, which eliminates the middle term in the final [equation for](Vector%20and%20Scalar%20Potentials.md) $\underline{A}$.
$$\underline{\nabla}\cdot \underline{A}'=\underline{\nabla}\cdot \underline{A}+\nabla^2\lambda$$
$$\mu_{0}\varepsilon_{0} \frac{\partial \phi'}{\partial t}=\mu_{0}\varepsilon_{0} \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial t}-\mu_{0}\varepsilon_{0} \frac{\partial^2\lambda}{\partial t^2}.$$
$$\underline{\nabla}\cdot \underline{A}'+\mu_{0}\varepsilon_{0} \frac{\partial \phi'}{\partial t}=\underline{\nabla}\cdot \underline{A}+\mu_{0}\varepsilon_{0} \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial t}+\nabla^2\lambda-\mu_{0}\varepsilon_{0} \frac{\partial^2\lambda}{\partial t^2}.$$
We take $\lambda$ s.t.
$$\nabla^2\lambda-\mu_{0}\varepsilon_{0} \frac{\partial^2\lambda}{\partial t^2}=-\underline{\nabla}\cdot \underline{A}-\mu_{0}\varepsilon_{0} \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial t}.$$
This gives that in the Lorenz gauge we have
$$\nabla^2\underline{A}-\mu_{0}\varepsilon_{9} \frac{\partial^2\underline{A}}{\partial t^2}=-\mu_{0}\underline{j}$$
$$\nabla^2\phi-\mu_{0}\varepsilon_{0} \frac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial t^2}=-\frac{\rho}{\varepsilon_{0}}.$$
