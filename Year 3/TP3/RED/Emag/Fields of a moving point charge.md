The retarded potentials in the [[Lorenz Gauge]] are given by
$$\phi(\underline{r},t)=\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_{0}}\int_{V} \frac{\rho(\underline{r}',t_{r})}{R}dV'$$
$$\underline{A}(\underline{r},t)=\frac{\mu_{0}}{4\pi}\int_{V} \frac{\underline{j}(\underline{r}',t_{r})}{R}dV'.$$
Consider a point charge moving along trajectory given by $w(t)$.
![[Pasted image 20241119191345.png]]
There is at most one point in $w(t)$ for the retarded time that contributes to the potentials at time $t$.
$$\lvert r-w(t) \rvert=c(t-t_{r})\implies t_{r}=t-\frac{{\lvert r-w(t) \rvert }}{c} .$$
Consider two points $t_{1},t_{2};t_{1}\neq t_{2}$.
The distance from the observer to point $P_1$ is $\underline{r}_{1}$, and the distance from the observer to point $P_{2}$ is $\underline{r}_{2}$.
$$R_{2}=c(t-t_{2});\;R_{1}=c(t-t_{1}).$$
$$R_{2}-R_{1}=c(t_{2}-t_{1})\implies \frac{{R_{2}-R_{1}}}{t_{1}-t_{2}}=c.$$
This implies the particle moves at $c$, but as charged particles cannot travel at the speed of light, there is only one point that contributes to the retarded time.
This retarded time lies on the boundary between [time-like](Basics%20of%20Relativity.md) and space-like events.
