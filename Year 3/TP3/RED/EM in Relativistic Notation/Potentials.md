Using [[4-Potential]], $A^{\mu}=(\phi,c\underline{A})$, we can rewrite the potential equations in relativistic notation.
The [[Gauge Transformations]], given by
$$\underline{A}'=\underline{A}+\underline{\nabla}\lambda$$
$$\phi'=\phi-\frac{\partial \lambda}{\partial t},$$
can be expressed in tensor notation as
$$A'^{\mu}=A^{\mu}-c\partial^{\mu}\lambda.$$
The [[Lorenz Gauge]] condition,
$$\underline{\nabla}\cdot \underline{A}=-\frac{1}{c^2} \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial t},$$
can be expressed in tensor notation as
$$\partial_{\mu}A^{\mu}=0.$$
The equations for $\underline{A}$ and $\phi$ in the Lorentz gauge are
$$-\nabla^2\phi+\frac{1}{c^2} \frac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial t^2}=\frac{\rho}{\varepsilon_{0}}$$
$$-\nabla^2\underline{A}+\frac{1}{c^2} \frac{\partial^2\underline{A}}{\partial t^2}=\mu_{0}\underline{j}.$$
Using the [[D'Alembert Operator]], $\square=\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\mu}$ these equations simplify to
$$\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\mu}A^{\nu}=\frac{1}{c\varepsilon_{0}}j^{\nu}.$$
