Consider a pole in frame $S$ that has a length $l_{0}$, with the positions of either end of the pole being $\underline{x}_{1}$ and $\underline{x}_{2}$ respectively.
![[Pasted image 20241019202911.png]]
Now consider frame $S'$ that is moving at speed $v$ with respect to $S$. To measure $l_{0}$ in this new frame, we need to measure $\underline{x}'_{1}$ and $\underline{x}_{2}'$ simultaneously, i.e. $t_{1}'=t_{2}'$.
Taking the [Lorentz transformation](Lorentz%20Transforms.md) of $\underline{x}_{1}-\underline{x}_{2}$ gives
$$l_{0}=\gamma(\underline{x}_{2}'+vt_{2}')-\gamma(\underline{x}_{1}'+vt_{1}')=\gamma(\underline{x}_{2}'-\underline{x}_{1}')-\gamma v(t_{2}'-t_{1}')=\gamma(\underline{x}_{2}'-\underline{x}_{1}')=\gamma l_{0}'.$$
$$\therefore l_{0}=\gamma l_{0}'.$$
Proper length is defined as the length measured in the rest frame of the pole.
