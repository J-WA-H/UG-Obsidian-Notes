Consider these two reference frames, with the second one (S') moving at speed $v$ relative to the first frame (S).

An event in frame S is determined by its position $(x,y,z)$ and time $t$. When describing in the event in the S' frame, a Lorentz transform is used, which in the $x$ direction is:
$$ct'=\gamma(ct-\beta x)$$
$$x'=\gamma(-\beta ct+x)$$
$$y'=y$$
$$z'=z$$
where $\beta=\frac{v}{c}$ and $\gamma=\frac{1}{\sqrt{ 1-\beta^2 }}$.

The Minkowski norm is a preserved quantity under a Lorentz transform. i.e:
$$c^2t^2-x^2-y^2-z^2=c^2t'^2-x'^2-y'^2-z'^2.$$
A Lorentz transform is defined as a transformation that preserves the Minkowski norm.
$$c^2t^2-x^2-y^2-z^2=\text{constant}.$$
