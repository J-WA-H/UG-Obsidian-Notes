For $\underline{x},\underline{y}\in E$ with $\alpha,\beta\in \mathbb{R}$, the scalar product $(\underline{x},\underline{y})$ of a pair of vectors assigns an element in $\mathbb{R}$ that satisfies
1. Linearity
	$$(\underline{x},\alpha \underline{y}+\beta \underline{y})=\alpha(\underline{x}, \underline{y})+\beta(\underline{x},\underline{y}).$$
2. Commutivity
	$$(\underline{x},\underline{y})=(\underline{y},\underline{x})$$
3. Positivity
	$$(\underline{x},\underline{x})\geq 0 \text{ for standard Euclidean geometry.}$$
	$(\underline{x},\underline{x})=0 \iff \underline{x}=\underline{0}$.

The scalar product of a vector with itself defines its norm
$$\lVert \underline{x} \rVert=\sqrt{ (\underline{x},\underline{x}) } .$$
Two orthogonal vectors have an inner product that equals 0.
