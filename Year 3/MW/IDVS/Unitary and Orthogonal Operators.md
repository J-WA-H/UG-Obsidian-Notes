An [operator](Linear%20Operators.md), $A$, in the complex space $\mathbb{C}^n\to \mathbb{C}^n$ over the field $\mathbb{C}$ is unitary if it preserves the scalar product
$$(A\underline{x},A\underline{y})=(\underline{x},\underline{y})\implies A^{\dagger}=A^{-1}.$$
The inverse of a unitary matrix is its [adjoint](Adjoint%20and%20Hermitian%20Operators.md). 

An operator in a real vector space $\mathbb{R}^n\to \mathbb{R}^n$ over the field $\mathbb{R}$ is orthogonal if it preserves the inner product.
$$(A\underline{x},A\underline{y})=(\underline{x},\underline{y})\implies A^T=A^{-1}.$$
The inverse of an orthogonal matrix is its transpose.

The unitary matrices form the group $U(n)$ and the orthogonal matrices form the group $O(n)$.

The determinant for a unitary matrix is $\lvert \det(U) \rvert=1$, and for an orthogonal matrix $\det(O)=\pm 1$.
The unitary matrices with $\det(U)=1$ for the special unitary group $SU(n)$, and the orthogonal matrices with $\det(O)=1$ form the group of special orthogonal matrices $SO(n)$.
