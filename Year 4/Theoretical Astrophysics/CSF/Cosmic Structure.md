The large scale structures in the universe are:
- Galaxies - gravitationally bound collection of stars, gas, and [dark matter](Contents%20of%20the%20Universe.md) - $\sim 1-100\text{ kpc}$
- Galaxy clusters - gravitationally bound collection of galaxies, gas, and dark matter - $\sim 100\text{ kpc}-1\text{ Mpc}$.
- Large scale systems - gravitationally unbound systems, expanding by [[Hubble Flow]] - $\sim1\text{ Mpc} - 1\text{ Gpc}$.

Hubble observed a 2D map of galaxies using angular positions and found the distribution to be non-uniform.
A 3D distribution of galaxies can be obtained by using redshift, $d=\frac{cz}{H}$. From this galaxies are observed to be clustered in gravitationally unbound structures on scales $\sim 1-1000\text{ Mpc}$. As galaxies are non-uniformly distributed, there are large scale structures formed with areas of high and low density.

The current history of the universe is believed to be:
- Big bang
- Inflation $10^{-43}-10^{-35}\text{ s}$
- Lepton era $10^{-35}-10^{-6}\text{ s}$ 
- Protons formed at $10^{-6}\text{ s}$
- Nucleon age $10^{-6}-255\text{ s}$
- Nuclear fusion begins $255\text{ s}$
- Age of nucleosynthesis
- Nuclear fusion ends
- ion age
- Recombination (CMB formed) $300,000\text{ yr}$
- Age of atoms
- First stars and galaxies form
- Age of stars and galaxies - today

Our current understanding implies the origin of structure in the universe is inflation and gravity. 
The current origin of structures is believed to be
- Seed fluctuations generated at the earliest instants of the big bang as quantum fluctuations in a scalar field that drives inflation
- These density fluctuations grow by self gravity
- The growth of these fluctuations is slowed by other effects (expansion, pressure, free-streaming)

The energy density at the start if inflation is dominated by a scalar field $\Phi$,
$$\rho=\frac{V(\Phi)}{c^2}\tag{2.1} $$
![[Pasted image 20251011170900.png]]
- $\Phi$ starts away from the minimum
- $\Phi$ decays towards the minimum keeping $\rho\sim\text{const.}$
- $\rho\sim\text{const.}$ drives exponential expansion, known as inflation

Inflation predicts the universe is spatially flat, homogeneous, and isotropic on large scales. Inflation expands initially tiny patch to the whole of the observable universe. The field $\Phi$ driving inflation has quantum fluctuations, which inflation expands to macroscopic scales. These become density fluctuations which seed growth to large scale structures.

The spectrum of the density fluctuations can be represented by a graph like
![[Pasted image 20251011171315.png]]
The spectrum can be given as a Fourier decomposition,
$$\delta(\vec{r})=\frac{\delta \rho}{\rho}=\sum_{k}\delta_{k}e^{ i\vec{k}\cdot \vec{r} }\tag{2.2} $$where
$$k=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}.\tag{2.3} $$
The power spectrum is then given by
$$P(k)\propto \langle |\delta_{k}|^2 \rangle .\tag{2.4} $$
Inflation generates density perturbations on all scales with a particular spectrum. Self gravity tends to make density perturbations grow in amplitude which generates gravitational instability. This instability is slowed by the expansion of the universe. There is simple growth on very large scales/early times, with just gravity and cosmological expansion only. The growth is more complicated on smaller scales, with pressure in baryons/photons generating oscillations and free-streaming of collisionless dark matter which generates damping.

From quantum fluctuations of $n\approx 1$ and the growth and damping from the nature of dark matter, we get the power spectrum
$$P(k)=Ak^{n}T^2(k,T)\tag{2.5} $$where $T(k,T)$ us the transfer function.

The type of structure formation is very different depending on the nature of dark matter - HDM gives no structure on small scales but CDM gives structure on all scales.

The CMB formed $\sim 380,000$ years after the big bang with a temperature of $T=10,000\text{ K}$ - while today it is $2.73\text{ K}$.

Temperature fluctuations in the CMB are of around $\frac{\Delta T}{T}=10^{-5}$, which gives the first evidence of density fluctuations.

To consider structure formed from the CMB we need:
- Structure originated from small density fluctuations
- Galaxies and clusters are highly non-linear structures
- Galaxy clustering on scales $<10\text{ Mpc}$ is also non-linear

Numerical simulations are needed to study non-linear evolution - N-body simulations since dark matter makes up most matter.

From the current $\Lambda$CDM model of the universe, the universe is flat with vacuum energy, most of the dark matter present is CDM and density fluctuations come from inflation.
