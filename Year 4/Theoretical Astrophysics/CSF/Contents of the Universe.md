We currently believe the universe is made of 75% dark energy, 21% dark matter, and 4% normal (baryonic) matter.

Define the [density parameter](Critical%20Density.md) which gives the fraction of matter that is a certain species,
$$\Omega=\frac{\rho}{\rho_{crit}}\tag{1.1} $$where $\rho_{crit}$ is the critical density - the density needed for a flat universe.

Photons emitted from the [[Cosmic Microwave Background]] at a temperature of $2.73\text{ K}$ have a density parameter $\Omega_{\gamma_{0}}=5.1\times 10^{-5}$. All three species of neutrinos have $\Omega_{\nu_{0}}=3.4\times 10^{-5}$ - assuming they're massless, and $\Omega_{\nu_{0}}=6\times 10^{-2}$ assuming massive.
Baryons have $\Omega_{b_{0}}=0.048$, of which $\Omega_{s_{0}}=0.0023$ is contained in stars.
Cold dark matter has the density parameter $\Omega_{dm_{0}}=0.26$ while dark energy has the parameter $\Omega_{\Lambda_{0}}=0.69$.
Today, $\Omega_{tot_{0}}=\Omega_{b_{0}}+\Omega_{dm_{0}}+\Omega_{\Lambda_{0}}\cong{1}$ so the universe is approximately flat.

Baryons come from [[Primordial Nucleosynthesis]], where nuclear reactions in the early universe produce light elements $(D,He,Li)$ when $T\sim 10^{9}\text{ K}$. The predicted and observed primordial deuterium abundance are in agreement with the abundance of the other light elements $({}^{3}_{}He,{}^{4}He,{}^{3}Li)$.

The best observational evidence for dark matter comes from galaxy rotation curves
![[Pasted image 20251009140043.png]]
The observed rotation curve and the curve determined by the stars and gas show there is a missing mass, which is attributed to dark matter.
Other evidence for dark matter comes from gravitational lensing, where the shapes of distant galaxies are distorted as light rays are deflected by gravity. Lensing detects more mass than is given by baryonic matter.
From dynamical and gravitational measurements, the total matter density is $\Omega_{m_{0}}\sim 0.3$. and from BBN, $\Omega_{b_{0}}\sim0.048$ so there is approximately five times more dark matter than baryonic matter so dark matter is not baryonic.

Dark matter could be produced in accelerators or be detected deep in mines where cosmic rays can't penetrate. Detectors can be a sample of Germanium and we try to observe the recoil energy and phonons of the interaction, although there have been no convincing detections yet.

Dark energy is represented by the cosmological constant $\Lambda$ with $\Omega_{\Lambda_{0}}\sim 0.7$. Dark energy produces a repulsive gravitational force with evidence from high-$z$ supernovae. Type Ia SN are standard candles and at $z\sim 0.5-1$ are fainter than expected even if the universe was empty. This implies cosmic expansion must have been accelerating since light was emitted, so there is some form of energy with negative pressure which causes a repulsive gravitational force.
