Consider the ground state of an $N$ electron system $\Phi^{(N)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N!} }\det[\phi_{1}^\sigma,\phi_{2}^\boldsymbol{\sigma}\dots,\phi_{k}^\sigma,\dots \phi_{N}^\sigma;\phi_{1}^\bar{\sigma},\dots,\phi_{2}^\bar{\sigma}]$. We ionise $\phi_{k}^\sigma$ so take it to $\infty$,
$$\therefore\Phi_{k^\sigma}^{(N-1)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{ (N-1)! }}\det[\phi_{1}^\boldsymbol{\sigma}.,\;,\dots,\phi_{N^\sigma}^\sigma;\phi_{1}^\bar{\sigma},\dots,\phi_{N^\bar{\sigma}}^\bar{\sigma}].\tag{5.1} $$
The total energy is given by
$$\bra{\Phi^{(N-1)}}H\ket{\Phi^{(N-1)}} -\bra{\Phi^{(N)}} H\ket{\Phi^{(N)}} =-\lambda_{k}^\sigma.\tag{5.2} $$
Usually we consider $\Phi^\sigma_{N^\sigma}$, which is the highest occupied molecular orbital - Homo.

Koopmans theorem states that the ionisation energy $\lambda_{k}^\sigma$ is given by the difference in energies of the $\Phi^{(N-1)}$ and $\Phi^{(N)}$.

The electron affinity, the energy released by the capture of an additional electron in $\phi^\sigma_{a}$ - the unoccupied eigenfunction of the [[Hartree-Fock Approximation]].
$$EA=\bra{\Phi^{(N)}} H\ket{\Phi^{(N)}} -\bra{\Phi_{a^{\sigma}}^{(N+1)}} H\ket{\Phi_{a^\sigma}^{(N+1)}}=-\varepsilon_{a}^\sigma \tag{5.3}.  $$
