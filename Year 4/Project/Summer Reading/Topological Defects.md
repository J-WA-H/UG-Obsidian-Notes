Studying the Goldstone model [(1.1)](Symmetry%20Breaking.md),$$\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\phi ^{\dagger})(\partial^{\mu}\phi)-V(\phi),\tag{1.1} $$where $\phi$ is a multi component scalar field transforming under a chosen global symmetry group $G$, this gives rise to many topological defects.
The most familiar defect is the $\phi^{4}$-kink, which arises in the Goldstone model with
$$V(\phi)=\frac{\lambda}{4}(\phi^2-\eta^2)^2.\tag{1.2} $$
Solving this gives a localised kink in the potential at $x=0$
![[Pasted image 20250704152914.png]]

The solution is time-independent, of finite energy, and Lorentz invariance so it is stable. This stability comes from the non-trivial topology of $\mathcal{M}$. On the left side, the minimum is at $\phi=-\eta$, and on the right side it is at $\phi=\eta$. The energy of the configuration is due to the localised departure of $\phi$ from the disconnected minima as it continuously interpolates between them, rising over the potential hill of the double well. The removal of this defect entails an infinite cost lifting all of the field over the kink which gives rise to the stability. This defect can be annihilated by an anti-defect having an opposite topological orientation.

From this, stability becomes a consequence of a topological conservation law, with each defect state corresponding to a conserved quantum number. This can be used to derive topological current
$$j^{\mu}=\epsilon^{\mu \nu}\partial_{\nu}\phi.\tag{6.1} $$
Current is conserved because it is a divergence of an antisymmetric tensor. The topological charge is then
$$N=\int dxj^{0}=\phi |_{x=+\infty}-\phi|_{x=-\infty}.\tag{6.2} $$
Non-zero charge indicates the stability of the configuration.

Although these conservation laws give the conditions for the existence of topological defects, it provides little information about the detailed structure or energy of a particular defect.
