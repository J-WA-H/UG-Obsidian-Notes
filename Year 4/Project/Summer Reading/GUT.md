A model of grand unified theory is given by $SO(10)$. The subgroups of interest are
$$SO(10)\to \begin{cases}
SU(5) & \text{Georgi-Glashow} \\
SU(4)\times SU(2)_{L}\times SU(2)_{R} & \text{Pati-Salam}
\end{cases}.\tag{8.1} $$
$SU(5)$ is the minimal $SO(10)$ model and $SU(2)_{L}$ is the left-handed [SU(2) group](Breaking%20SU(2)%20Symmetry.md) which only acts on left handed chirality particles.

Strictly this should be described as $Spin(10)$, the simply connected covering group for $SO(10)$, as spinor representations will be considered.

The generators of $Spin(10)$ are given by
$$\sigma_{ij}=\frac{1}{2i}[\Gamma_{j},\Gamma_{k}];\;j,k=1,2,\dots,\tag{8.2} 3$$
where $\Gamma_{j}$ are the 10 dimensional Dirac matrices - $32\times{3}2$ matrices constructed from direct products of the Pauli-matrices. 

Each family of left-handed fermions belongs to a 16-dimensional spinor representation, denoted by $\mathbf{16}$. These families transform under the symmetry $SU(5)\times U(1)_{P}\in Spin(10)$, having the $SU(5)$ decomposition
$$\mathbf{16}\to 1_{5}+10_{1}+5_{-3}\tag{8.3} $$
where the subscripts are the eigenvalues of the operator
$$P=\sigma_{12}+\sigma_{34}+\sigma_{56}+\sigma_{78}+\sigma_{9\;10}.\tag{8.4} $$
The decomposition $(8.3)$ corresponds to the usual $SU(5)$ particle assignments plus a neutral singlet which plays the role of a right-handed neutrino.
Note that the charge operator is given by
$$\tag{8.5} Q=\frac{1}{3}(\sigma_{12}+\sigma_{34}+\sigma_{56})-\sigma_{9\;10}. $$
The symmetry breaking in the Higgs representations are considered to be formed from direct products, here from $\mathbf{16}$ and its conjugate $\mathbf{\overline{16}}$,
$$\tag{8.6} \begin{matrix}
(\mathbf{16}\times \mathbf{16})_{S}=\mathbf{10}+\mathbf{126}, \\
\mathbf{16}\times \overline{16}=\mathbf{1}+\mathbf{45}+\mathbf{210},
\end{matrix}$$
where the subscript $S$ denotes the symmetric product. The decompositions of these are
$$\tag{8.7} 
\begin{matrix}
\mathbf{126}\to 1_{10}+10_{6}+50_{2}+\bar{5}_{2}+\overline{45}_{2}+\overline{45}_{-2}+\overline{15}_{-6}, \\
\mathbf{45}\to \overline{10}_{4}+24_{0}+1_{0}+10_{-4}, \\

\mathbf{10}\to \overline{5}_2+5_{-2}.
\end{matrix}$$

[[Symmetry Breaking]] proceeds first with a Higgs field in the $\mathbf{126}$-representation acquiring a non-zero vacuum expectation value $\phi_{\mathbf{126}}$ lying in the direction of the $1_{10}$ component. Phenomenological constraints require that this occurs at a superheavy mass scale $\sim 10^{16}-10^{17}\text{ GeV}$, followed by another Higgs in the adjoint $\mathbf{45}$-representation acquiring vacuum expectation value (VEV) at approximately $10^{14}\text{ GeV}$. Finally, at the electroweak scale, a further Higgs field in the $\mathbf{10}$-representation acquires a non-zero VEV to give the overall scheme
$$\tag{8.8} \begin{matrix}
Spin(10)\xrightarrow{\mathbf{126}} SU(5)\times \mathbb{Z}_{2} \\
\xrightarrow{\mathbf{45}} SU(3)_{c}\times SU(2)_{L}\times U(1)_{Y} \\
\xrightarrow{\mathbf{10}} SU(3)_{c}\times U(1)_{Q}\times \mathbb{Z}_{2}.
\end{matrix} $$
The unbroken $\mathbb{Z}_{2}$ symmetry which merges at the first stage is generated by rotations through $2\pi$ in the $P$-direction, that is, by $\exp{\left(\frac{2\pi iP}{1o}\right)}$. This is because $\exp \left( \frac{2\pi iP}{5} \right)$ is contained in $SU(5)$. Not that the hypercharge $Y$ belongs to the $24_{0}$ of the $\mathbf{45}$ and so commutes with the rotations by $\exp\left( \frac{2\pi iP}{19} \right)$, while the $\mathbf{10}$-representation has no components with eigenvalue $P=0$ as seen in $(8.7)$.

Another way to get from $SO(10)$ to the standard model, the simplest way uses a Higgs field in the $\mathbf{16}$-representation, $Spin(10)\to SU(5)$. In this case there is no discrete symmetry and the unbroken subgroup is the global $SU(5)$. Alternatively, we can introduce an earlier stage of symmetry breaking with an additional Higgs field in the $\mathbf{45}$ or $\mathbf{210}$ representations going first through the maximal subgroup $Spin(10)\to SU(5)\times U(1)_{P}$.
