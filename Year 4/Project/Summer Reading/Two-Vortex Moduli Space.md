At [critical coupling](Abelian%20Higgs%20Model.md) the energy of $n$-vortex solutions is bounded below by $2\pi |n|$. The set of static solutions which achieve this bound is determined by choosing $n$ points in $\mathbb{R}^{2}$, the locations of the $n$ zeros of the Higgs field. The static $n$-vortex solution space is then a $2|n|$-dimensional submanifold, or moduli space $M_{n}$ within all field configuration space.

The moduli space $M_{n}$ corresponds to degenerate energy minima for the configuration space of all time-dependent solutions with the same winding number $n$. A slowly moving $n$-vortex configuration will therefore lie close in field space to $M_n$. Conservation of energy then implies that its trajectory must always remain confined near $M_n$, since this is a global minimum of the potential energy. For the same reason, in the vicinity of $M_n$ gradients of the potential energy are small and the dynamics will be governed by the kinetic energy. These kinetic terms, $\vec{\dot{A}}\cdot \vec{\dot{{A}}}$, $\dot{\phi}\dot{\bar{\phi}}$, in the Lagrangian $\mathcal{L}$ induces a metric on $M_n$ and, in the absence of potential terms, the variational principal implies that motion will be geodesic on $M_n$. Non-trivial scattering is possible only because $M_n$ is, in general, a curved submanifold.

In a two-vortex case, the appropriate coordinates describing the vortex motion are the two collective coordinates describing their overall translations and a further two describing their relative motion, that is, $M_{2}\cong \mathbb{R}^2\times M_{2}^{0}$. Only the latter is of interest, so we consider the metric $g_{ij}$ on this two dimensional subspace $M_{2}^{0}$. Rotation and parity invariance constrain this metric to take the form
$$ds^2=F^2(r)dr^2+G^2(r)d\theta^2,\tag{13.1} $$where $(r\cos \theta,r\sin \theta)$ is half the separation vector of the two vortices. Since these configurations are equivalent under a $180^{\circ}$ rotation, this suggests that $\theta$ should be identified by $\pi$ rather than $2\pi$, creating the bound $0\leq\theta<\pi$. At large $r$ the vortices do not interact and the metric must be flat,
$$ds^2\approx dr^2+r^2d\theta^2.\tag{13.2} $$
For small $r$ when the two vortices almost coincide, the two-vortex system can be viewed as a perturbation about a single vortex of winding number $n=2$. Near the origin the metric on $M_{2}^{0}$ takes the form
$$\tag{13.3} ds^2\approx(c_{0}r)^2(ds^2+r^2d\theta^2).$$Under a coordinate transform $\rho=\frac{c_{0}r^2}{2}$ the metric becomes
$$ds^2\approx d\rho^2+4\rho^2d\theta^2,\tag{13.4} $$
and we see that, with $\theta$ identified by $\pi$, $M_{2}^{0}$ is a smooth manifold with no conical singularity at $r=0$. $M_{2}^{0}$ can be represented as a surface of revolution in $\mathbb{R}^3$, which approaches a cone of semi-angle $\frac{\pi}{6}$ at large $r$ and which becomes smoothed out at small $r$. A geodesic corresponding to a head-on collision passes over a smoothed peak of this cone and thus $\theta$ changes by $\frac{\pi}{2}$ given its identification by $\pi$. This is the prediction of right-angled scattering.

The simplest way to observe right-angled scatting for a head-on collision is to consider the fluctuations, corresponding to a two-vortex splitting mode. For one of these orthogonal modes, the Higgs field takes the form
$$\phi(\vec{r})=f(r)e^{ 2i\theta }+\kappa f(r)h(r),\tag{13.5} $$
where $h\approx f^{-1}$ near $r=0$, and $\kappa$ parametrises the vortex separation. With $\kappa$ negative but increasing, it is trivial to show that the zeros lie on the $x$-axis and approach the origin. When $\kappa$ changes sign, the zeros meet the origin but then separate along the $y$-axis. It is interesting to note that the position of the zeros is constrained to move as the square root of the energy density, $r\approx \sqrt{ \kappa }$, unlike at large separations where $r\approx \kappa$ to high precision. This implies that the zeros move superluminally during close vortex encounters, which is consistent with the fact that the metric function $F(r)$ in $(13.1)$ vanishes at the origin. The ring-like distribution of the energy density when the vortices superpose make the superluminal motion of the central zeros of little energetic consequence.

Right-angled scattering was observed to be generic for all head-on collisions, irrespective of coupling or velocity. In the weakest generalisation, the [[Inter-Vortex Potential]] becomes flat as the two vortices near superposition regardless of coupling. Hence, with a vanishing interaction force, at small separations the vortices will move in what approximates a degenerate moduli space. At large separations, slowly moving vortices are confined as before near the base of a steep potential valley in field configuration space. For $\beta\neq 1$, the submanifold of these minima is no longer degenerate in energy and the configuration accelerate or decelerate depending on the sign of $\beta-1$. With $\beta\sim 1$ near critical it should be possible to reproduce this motion merely by introducing a small potential on the original moduli space $M_{2}^{0}$.
