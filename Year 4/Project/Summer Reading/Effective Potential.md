The previous examples of symmetry breaking has used a purely classical potential to find the expectation of the Higgs field. In reality, $\phi$ is a quantum field interacting with itself and other quantum fields. This means $V(\phi)$ needs to be modified for a more accurate expectation value. The corrected potential is the effective potential
$$V_{eff}(\phi)=V(\phi)+V_{1}(\phi)+V_{2}(\phi)+\dots \tag{4.1} $$where $V_{n}(\phi)$ is the contribution of Feynman diagrams with $n$ closed loops. In some models the radiative corrections are negligible, while in others they completely alter the nature of symmetry breaking.
For example, consider the Higgs model with
$$V(\phi)=\mu_{0}^2|\phi|^2.\tag{4.2} $$
Radiative corrections due to the gauge coupling generate a completely different effective potential, with the one-loop correction being
$$V_{1}(\phi)=\frac{3e^4}{16\pi^2}|\phi|^4\ln\left( \frac{|\phi|^2}{\sigma^2} \right)\tag{4.3} $$
where $\sigma$ is a renormalisation constant.

Introducing the dimensionless quantity
$$\nu=\frac{16\pi^2\mu_{0}^2}{3e^4\sigma^2},\tag{4.4} $$
it can be shown that $\nu>0.45$ $V_{eff}(\phi)$ has a single minimum at $\phi=0$ and for $\nu<0.45$ there is another minimum at a non-zero value of $\phi$, and for $\nu<0.37$ this minimum is deeper than the one at $\phi=0$. In this latter case the absolute minimum is at $\phi\neq 0$ so the symmetry is spontaneously broken.

For the general gauge model the one-loop correction is
$$V_{1}(\phi)=\frac{1}{64\pi^2}\left[ \mathrm{Tr}\left( \mu^4\ln \frac{\mu^2}{\sigma^2} \right)+3\mathrm{Tr}\left( M^4\ln \frac{M^2}{\sigma^2} \right)-4\mathrm{Tr}\left( n^4\ln \frac{m^2}{\sigma^2} \right) \right]\tag{4.5} $$
where $\mu,M$ and $m$ are the scalar, vector, and spinor mass matrices respectively.

Using the Higgs and Yukawa couplings, $\lambda$ and $\chi$, $V_{1}(\phi)$ is a small correction if
$$\lambda\ll 1;\;e^4\ll\lambda;\;\chi^4\ll\lambda.\tag{4.6} $$
The effective potential is not necessarily gauge invariant but the value of $V$ at the minimum is. $V_{eff}$ can also be complex, but this does not affect the minimum.
