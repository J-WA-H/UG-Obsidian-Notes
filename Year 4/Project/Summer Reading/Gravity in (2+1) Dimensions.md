$(2+1)$-dimensional gravity is closely related to [straight string metrics](Linearised%20Gravity.md). If the $dz^2$ term in $(19.12)$ is removed, we obtain a solution of the Einstein equations for a point mass in $(2+1)$ dimensions. In $(2+1)$ dimensions, the Weyl tensor vanishes and the Riemann tensor is completely determined by the Ricci tensor,
$$\tag{20.1} R^{\mu \nu}_{\;\;\;\sigma \tau}=\epsilon^{\mu \nu\alpha}\epsilon_{\sigma \tau \beta}\left( R^{\beta}_{\;\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}\delta^{\beta}_{\;\alpha}R \right).$$
Einstein's equations require that outside of any sources $R_{\mu \nu}=0$, and thus spacetime is flat. This implies there are no gravitational waves and there is no Newtonian gravity in $(2+1)$ dimensions.
The metric for a spinning point source in $(2+1)$ dimensions in spatially conformal coordinates is
$$ds^2=(dt+4GJd\theta)^2-R^{-8G\mu}(dR^2+R^2d\theta^2), \tag{20.2} $$
where $\mu,J=\text{const}$, and the energy-momentum tensor is given by
$$\begin{matrix}
\sqrt{ g }\;T^{00}=\mu\delta^{(2)}(\vec{x}), & T^{ij}, \\
\sqrt{ g }\;T^{0i}=\frac{1}{2}J\epsilon^{ij}\partial_{j}\delta^{(2)}(\vec{x}).
\end{matrix}\tag{20.3} $$
The angular momentum of the source is
$$J=\epsilon_{ij}\int d^2x\sqrt{ g }\;x^{i}T^{0j}.\tag{20.4} $$
With a new radial coordinate, $r=(1-4G\mu)^{-1}R^{1-4G\mu}$, the metric in $(20.2)$ becomes
$$ds^2=(dt-4GJd\theta)^2-dr^2-(1-4G\mu)^{2}d\theta^2\tag{20.5}  .$$
It is now easy to see that outside the source this metric is flat and can be brought to the Minkowski form by a coordinate transform $t'=t+4GJ\theta,\theta'=(1-4G\mu)\theta$.
The spacetime outside an arbitrary distribution of particles in the centre of mass frame can also be described by this metric. If particles are concentrated in a region $r<r_{0}$ around the origin, then the metric holds for $r>r_{0}$. In this case, $\mu$ is the total energy of the system and $J$ is the total angular momentum which may be due to both internal and orbital momenta of the particles. In the limit of weak gravity when all deficit angles are small, $\mu$ is just the sum of particle energies but, in the general case, the calculation of $\mu$ is more complicated.
A property of the metric in $(20.2)$ is that it admits closed time-like curves. Consider a curve of constant $t=t_{0}$ and $r=r_{0}$ with $\theta$ changing from $0$ to $2\pi$. This curve is obviously closed and also time-like provided $r_{0}<4GJ(1-4G\mu)^{-1}$. However, it can be shown that closed time-like curves are not present in a spacetime describing any system of spinless particles with a total deficit angle smaller than $2\pi$. In the $(3+1)$ case, closed time-like curves cannot evolve in a finite region of spacetime if the energy-momentum tensor satisfies the weak energy condition, $T_{\mu \nu}\ell^{\mu}\ell^{\nu}\geq 0$, for an arbitrary null-vector $\ell^{\mu}$. This condition is satisfied for cosmic strings, and thus strings cannot be used to build time machines.
