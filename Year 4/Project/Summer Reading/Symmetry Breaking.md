Symmetry breaking is described by scalar fields known as Higgs fields. The ground state does not exhibit all symmetries of the Hamiltonian.
The key features of spontaneous symmetry breaking are given by the Lagrangian
$$\mathcal{L}=(\partial_{\mu}\phi ^{*})(\partial^\mu \phi)-V(\phi) \tag{1.1}$$where $\phi$ is the complex scalar field. The potential is given by
$$V(\phi)=\frac{1}{4}\lambda(\phi ^{*}\phi-\eta^2)^2\tag{1.2}$$with $\lambda,\eta$ being positive constant.

The scalar field is invariant under the global $U(1)$ group,
$$\phi(x)\to e^{ i\alpha }\phi(x)\tag{1.3}$$where global states that $\alpha$ is independent of time.

The ground state lies on the circle $|\phi|=\eta$ so the ground state expectation value of the field is
$$\bra{0} \phi \ket{0} =\eta e^{ i\theta }.\tag{1.4}$$
$\theta$ is an arbitrary phase. The $U(1)$ transformation moves $\theta\to\theta+\alpha$ so the ground state $\ket{0}$ is not invariant under $U(1)$ so the symmetry is broken.

Another important symmetry is gauge theories with spontaneously broken symmetries. The abelian-Higgs model is the simplest model with the Lagrangian
$$\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{D}_{\mu}^{*}\phi ^{*}\mathcal{D}^\mu \phi-V(\phi)-\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu \nu}F^{\mu \nu}\tag{1.5}$$
where $\mathcal{D}_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}-ieA_{\mu}$ with $e$ the gauge coupling and $A_{\mu}$ is the gauge vector field.

This is also invariant under $U(1)$ of local gauge transformations,
$$\phi(x)\to e^{ i\alpha(x) }\phi(x),\;A_{\mu}(x)\to A_{\mu}(x)+e^{ -1 }\partial_{\mu}\alpha(x). \tag{1.6}$$
Like for global transformations, the potential minima are at $|\phi|=\eta$ so this symmetry is spontaneously broken with a non-zero expectation value.
